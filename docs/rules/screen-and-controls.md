# Screen and controls

- **Top bar:** the game clock, game speed, "Skip to next event", the Funding and Science balances, the Launch manifest, Research Tasks and Tech Tree buttons, the Guide and the Mission Director's Report, and Save and Load.
- **Status panel (left):** the current Act and Milestones, the Mission Director, the base's power, stock and bulk resources, the Lander and Expeditions, and the running Research Task, those queued behind it in order and, once the base has a telescope, the observing programme, above the build menu. Its lines are kept short: the Living line says where the Mission Director lives and whether the Habitat has power, the Components in stock are listed two to a row, and a figure that needs its reason, such as a stock that is rising but won't last the Night, keeps the reason for hovering over the line. At its top, while anything needs you, it lists what does, most urgent first, and nothing otherwise: first whatever its own lines warn of (a Solar Particle Event, the Habitat's power, oxygen and water, the Radiation Dose, spare Components), then anything Broken, Constructions waiting for Components that aren't in the base's stock, Plants standing idle for want of an input, Structures, Machines and Rovers below 40% Condition, and last the Plants you have switched off, in one line. Several of a kind share a line. Each line is a button that leads to where it is dealt with: its line further down the panel, unfolding its section, or the thing itself on the map, as its name in the Base panel does, the first of several or the lowest on Condition; pointing at such a line marks it on the map without moving the camera, as pointing at a row of the Base panel does. A Robot low on Condition isn't listed, as it repairs itself. The list says what still stands, where the notifications say what happened as it happens, and a line stays a few seconds, dimmed, once its cause is over.
- **Base panel (right):** the Robots, Machines and Rovers, and below them the Structures, with their Condition, between the Work panel and the notification list. Structures are listed by what they are for, under a heading with how many there are and what matters about them (how many are Broken, how many Plants are switched off, and the lowest Condition among the rest), and each group folds away on its own, as the sections do; a dot by a group's heading means one of them is Broken or low on Condition. Structures of one type that only stand and work, such as solar arrays, battery banks, lights and tanks, share one row once there are two of them: it gives how many there are, the lowest Condition among them and how many are Broken, without power, to be dismantled or due Maintenance, and opens to show a row for each. A Plant, a radio telescope array and a Habitat, with its shielding and water wall under it, keep a row each. A row says what state its Structure is in only when there is something to say: one that is commissioned and working takes a single line, with its name and Condition. Each row's name is a button that finds it on the map: the camera moves to it, keeping its angle and coming in closer only from far off, a ring marks it there for a few seconds, and for a Structure its pop-up opens, so that rows with the same name can be told apart. Pointing at a row, or moving the keyboard focus into it, marks it without moving the camera. A Robot or the Rover away on an Expedition isn't on the map, and the game says so.
- **Work panel (right, above the Base panel):** each Work Order with its stage, its progress and what is happening on it, under a heading with how many there are and how many are waiting. Work that can't go on until the base has something it lacks (a Construction's Components or water, spare Components for Maintenance), until a Broken Machine has been maintained or until the Robot is back from an Expedition is marked by a bar down its side and says why in the warning colour, and is listed below the work under way. Waiting for the Robot, or for a Machine at work on another site, doesn't count: the base's own work comes round to it.
- Each section of the status, Work and Base panels folds away when you click its title and stays folded on your next visit; a folded status section keeps its headline figures beside its title, such as the power to spare, how long the oxygen and water last, and which bulk resource will run out soonest, and a dot by a folded title means something in it needs attention. Hover over a self-sufficiency figure or the time left in the Lunation for detail.
- **Lines hold still, even at 20x.** Rows keep their order, a Work Order's moving only as it becomes held up or free to go on, and a line keeps the tallest height it has taken while shown, so that when its text gets shorter, however quickly or slowly, the lines below it never move back up; it gives the room back when its section is folded, a Campaign is loaded or the window changes size. Work that is done (a Construction commissioned, a Structure dismantled, Maintenance finished, a Machine or the Rover parked and plugged in, the Lander unloaded or loaded) stays in the Work panel, marked "Done", for a few real seconds before its row goes, while work that stops for any other reason, such as a cancelled Construction, goes at once; the Work panel stays in place saying "No work under way" while there is none, and the Solar Particle Event line keeps its place in the Mission Director section, saying "none announced" between events. A line with nothing to say is left out rather than kept to say so: the next Milestone's line goes for good once none is left, and the Expedition's line, the Research queue's and the Science earned a day aren't there until the first Expedition is under way, the first Research Task queued or Science first left unspent, and the first Science being earned. Once one has shown it stays, saying "none under way", "none" or "none being earned" between times, as a bulk resource's line does, so that the lines below it move only that once. The current Milestone's hint shows its first two lines, with a "Show all" button under it for the rest that the keyboard reaches too; it stays as the player leaves it, folded or whole, as the Milestones go by, so that it changes height only when they ask. A bulk resource's line, once it has shown, stays until a Campaign is loaded, even with none left.
- **Guide:** explains, without leaving the game, how the base works (power and Night, repairs, keeping the Mission Director alive, Launches and Funding, Science), what every Structure does, with its figures, Components and the Design it needs, what every Component is for, what the Robot, Machines and Rover do, and what each technology unlocks. Every figure and list in it comes from the data catalogue.
- **Mission Director's Report:** the button beside the Guide opens it. The Mission Director says, in their own words, what pleases them at the base and what concerns them, as things stand, and it keeps up as the game runs on. It reads only what the status panel shows, by the same warning levels: where they live and how well it keeps out radiation (aboard the Lander they are safe, but it can't fly; an unshielded Habitat concerns them; Regolith shielding, a water wall or a Buried Habitat pleases them, the more the better), the Radiation Dose taken (only a tenth of the limit pleases them, half of it or more concerns them) and any Solar Particle Event announced; whether the Habitat's power lasts the Night; each of oxygen and water, which concerns them once it is in warning or lasts 30 Earth Days or less, and pleases them while the base makes all they use, keeps it topped up or it is rising; the spare Components, which concern them once in warning, short of the repairs already waiting or lasting 30 Earth Days or less, and please them while the base makes them as fast as things wear or the stock lasts longer; anything Broken, below 40% Condition, or a Construction or Plant waiting, as the needs-attention list names it; Funding too little for a Launch's fee; whether the base earns Science; and the Milestones reached. A side with nothing on it says so.
- **What things are for, where you choose them:** hovering over a Structure in the build menu says what it does and what it is built from; each item on the Launch manifest says what it is for (the Structures built from a Component and how many each takes, what spare Components do, how long oxygen or water lasts the Mission Director); picking a Design on the Tech Tree says what unlocking it brings, with what each of its Structures does; and the Research Tasks screen lists what Science buys next, and on each task the Designs its Science would bring within reach or, if it pays too little for any, the cheapest it builds towards and how far short it leaves you. These say the same as the Guide.
- **Structure pop-up:** click a Structure on the map, commissioned, under Construction or being dismantled, or its name in the Base panel, to open a pop-up beside it saying what it is, what it does and what state it is in: its stage of Construction or dismantling and how far it has got, its Condition and any Maintenance, what it is rated to generate, store and draw and what its power network makes and uses now, a battery bank's charge, what a storage tank holds, a Plant's recipe and what it makes, a Habitat's residents, shielding and Radiation Dose rate, a light's state, whether a telescope is observing and how the observing programme is going, how far a Greenhouse's round has got, how many of a parking area's bays are taken, and a solar array's Dust coating. It updates live, and once the Structure is commissioned it offers the same choices as its entry in the Base panel (recipe, switch off or on, Move in or Move here), and Dismantle, which only the pop-up has. Clicking another Structure switches to it; clicking empty ground, pressing Esc or its close button closes it, and so does the Structure going once dismantled. A drag moves the camera rather than counting as a click, and while a Structure is being placed, a click places it instead. Regolith shielding and a water wall cover the Habitat, so clicking any of them opens the Habitat's pop-up, with a button to switch to each of them, and one back from theirs.
- **The desktop app's window:** it has no menu bar. Starting the game while it is already running brings its window to the front rather than opening a second one, and links to outside pages open in your default browser.
- **Exit (desktop app):** the Exit button at the right of the top bar closes the game, as the window's close button does, so it asks first whether to save any unsaved progress. It is the way out in fullscreen, where the window has no close button.
- **Fullscreen (desktop app):** press F11, or the Full screen button at the right of the top bar, to fill the screen, and either again to go back to a window. The button shows pressed while the game fills the screen. Esc never leaves fullscreen, even with nothing open: it closes the structure pop-up, the screens and the celebration, and cancels placing a Structure, so pressing it once too often can't throw you out of the game's screen. The game opens next time as you left its window: its size and place, maximised or in fullscreen. A window left on a screen that has since been unplugged opens on the main screen.
- **Camera:** drag with the left mouse button to pan, drag with the right button to rotate, and use the wheel to zoom from the whole map down to a single Robot.
