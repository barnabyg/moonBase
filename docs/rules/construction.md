# Construction

To plan a Construction, pick a Structure in the build menu, move its translucent preview over the map and click to place it; right-click or Esc cancels. The build menu lists the Structures whose Designs are unlocked under the same groups as the Base panel, by what they are for, and each group folds away, staying folded on your next visit; beside its name is how many items the base's stock could build now, the sum of the numbers beside its Structures, leaving out those made from Regolith alone, so a group with nothing in stock for it shows 0. Beside each Structure is how many the base's stock can supply, or a dash for one made from Regolith alone, and one the stock can't supply is set back, dimmed and with a dashed border, though it can still be planned, to wait for its Components. Nothing in it moves as the stock changes. The Robot carries it out by itself through the stages of Construction, and the Work panel shows each one:

- **Site Preparation:** the Robot drives the excavator to the site and activates it, and the excavator grades the ground by itself while the Robot gets on with other work. For a Structure the regolith printer prints, such as Regolith shielding or a sintered landing pad, it also digs the Regolith the printer will use, 6 hours more, so printed Structures cost excavator time and wear rather than Components or the base's Regolith stock. This is part of Site Preparation, not the digging that fills the stock (below).
- **Component delivery and Assembly:** the Robot loads the Components at the stockpile beside the Lander, carries them over and assembles them.
- **Commissioning**, which can fail.
- **Cable:** every Construction after the first that uses power also has the Robot lay power cable from the nearest powered Structure (one on a network with a solar array or battery bank). It pays the cable out along the way it walks, so the cable goes round anything in the way. Only Structures joined by cable share power. If a Structure that uses power is ever left without a source, the Robot lays it a new cable as soon as there is one.

A Construction whose Components aren't in the base's stock waits, with the reason in the Work panel, and the Robot moves on to the next. "Cancel" in the Work panel removes an unfinished Construction at once and returns its unassembled Components. "Dismantle" in a commissioned Structure's pop-up, which clicking it on the map or its name in the Base panel opens, has the Robot take it apart and carry spare Components back to the stockpile, rather than what it was built from: one from most Structures, and more from a large or complex one, up to six from a Crew habitat, while one made from Regolith alone gives nothing back. Its description in the build menu and the Guide, and the Dismantle button's tooltip, say how many. The Structure keeps working until the Robot starts. Its cables go with it, but anything that drew power through it stays on the network: its cables are joined up as it goes, so dismantling a battery bank or a solar array never cuts the Habitat off from the rest of the base's power.

Robots, and the Machines and Rover they drive, take the shortest way round the Lander and every Structure's footprint, keeping half a metre clear of it. That includes Structures still under Construction, from the moment they are planned, so that nobody crosses ground being graded or built on, and Structures being dismantled, until they are taken apart. They only step onto a footprint where their work is, such as beside the Lander on a landing pad, and make their way out of one, and round it, if they ever find themselves inside it. Squeezing between two footprints takes a gap of more than a metre for a Robot, and about 4 to 5 m for a Machine or the Rover. If Structures close a spot in completely, with no gap wide enough, whoever is going there goes straight through them rather than never arriving: leave gaps when you pack Structures round a courtyard.

Nothing can be built over the stockpile, the Lander, the landing site or the Rover, and while you place a Structure, the build menu shows the Plume Ejecta danger zone round the Lander (placing in it is allowed). A few Structures can't be dismantled while something depends on them: the Habitat the Mission Director lives in, a shielded Habitat until its shielding is gone, Regolith shielding until any water wall over it is gone, a landing pad while the Lander stands on it, and a radio telescope array while any antenna field joins it.

**Decorations.** Four Structures are there for how they look and what they mean, not for what they do: a flag pole, a founding memorial, a rock garden and the Mission Director's viewing area, a chair behind a windbreak looking out over the base (the Mission Director doesn't come out to it). They belong to the Decorations Design, a starting Construction Design, and the Base panel and build menu list them last, under Decoration. None draws power, gives light, takes a cable or earns anything, and their description in the build menu and the Guide says "It is there for how it looks and what it means, and does nothing else." The flag pole, memorial and viewing area each come by Launch as one kit (a flag kit of 20 kg, a memorial plaque of 30 kg and a viewing area kit of 35 kg, each $1M) and go up in 1¾ to 2¾ hours of work, never failing Commissioning; dismantling one gives back a spare Component. The rock garden is made from Regolith alone: the excavator grades its ground and digs its rocks in 3 hours, and the Robot sets them round a bed of Regolith in 4 more; dismantled, it gives nothing back. Dust wears the shipped three at 0.004 percentage points an hour, a fifth of a Light Mast's rate, and the rock garden not at all, but Micrometeorites and Plume Ejecta strike them as they do anything else, and the Robot maintains them as it does any Structure, with spare Components. Broken, a Decoration does nothing different, but it shows as Broken in the Base panel and the needs-attention list until it is repaired.
