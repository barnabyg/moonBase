# Balancing

The data catalogue's numbers are balanced against the reference Campaign's timeline, so that a new player can play from the landing to a self-sufficient, export-funded base by following the Milestones, and carry on afterwards. Every balancing change is in the data catalogue; none is in code.

The [reference Campaign](reference-campaign.md) and its commands are how these numbers are measured.

## Targets

The spec's tunable defaults all hold:

- 1x is 6 game-minutes a second.
- A Lunation is 20 Earth Days of Daylight and 9.5 of Night, and the first Night falls on Earth Day 8.
- The Lander's legs take 3, 4 and 3 Earth Days.
- The Robot repairs itself below 10% Condition.
- The starting oxygen and water last about 30 Earth Days.

The pacing targets, and where the reference Campaign came at data catalogue version 65:

At data catalogue version 66, as the pacing report prints, the Research Tasks pay 14,398 Science over 2,965 Earth Days back to back, and the Designs cost 12,325: 2,073 to spare, most of it from the last Research Tasks, which pay with nothing left to buy. These are the totals to balance against; the sections under [The larger changes, and why](#the-larger-changes-and-why) give those of their own time.

The Radiation Dose limit behind a water wall was last played to at data catalogue version 61, with no Buried Habitat: the Mission Director died of it on Earth Day 1,765, against the game's forecast of Earth Day 1,796, read as the long-game play guide then ended on Earth Day 1,650. Neither date has been re-measured since, as the guide now moves into a Buried Habitat first, and the Solar Particle Events fall differently whenever what it plays changes. Plan on the played date rather than the forecast, which counts only the Solar Particle Events the Mission Director knows of, and so comes late. The played date may now be some weeks early: at version 64 the guide reached 558.0 mSv on Earth Day 1,593, which at 0.197 mSv an Earth Day works out at about Earth Day 1,806, a sum rather than a played date.

The Campaign's content is to last about 10 real hours at 20x (3,000 Earth Days, as an hour at 20x is 300 Earth Days), in four Acts (ADR-0008). All four have their content, though Acts 3 and 4 have been played only by the long-game play guide, and the last hours don't yet have the purpose Act 4 is meant to give them (#179). ## The larger changes, and why

**Battery banks.** A Night lasts 228 hours. Through it the Habitat draws 274 kWh (1.2 kW), each solar array 11 kWh, and the heaters of the excavator, the regolith printer and the Rover, which the Robot plugs in before every Night, 137 kWh between them. A battery bank stores 200 kWh, about what a small electric car carries, and its heaters draw 0.07 kW, 16 kWh through a Night. The starting stock holds three battery banks, 600 kWh, which carry the Habitat, two solar arrays and all the heaters through a Night, about 480 kWh, with over 100 kWh to spare; two would not. The large battery bank stays the better buy: 800 kWh for 0.2 kW of heaters, less mass and money for each kWh. (A battery bank used to store 60 kWh, and its heaters to draw 0.25 kW, 57 of those 60 through a Night, so no number of battery banks could carry anything else through one.)

The Habitat's first Night has to be carried by the starting stock. The Lander can't leave until the Mission Director has moved in, and a round trip takes 10 Earth Days. Large battery banks need Science, and the Milestones put the first Research Task after the landing pad, which the first Launch brings, so they could arrive at the earliest on the second Launch, as the Night falls and uncharged. Staying aboard the Lander through the first Night only puts the problem off to the second. The first two Milestone hints say to build all the starting battery banks and to move in once they are charged, so a player following them gets through the first Night. The starting stock's four Light Masts would draw over 360 kWh through it, more than is left once the Habitat and the heaters have theirs, so the lights switch themselves off part-way through rather than leave the Habitat without power: building them costs a new player some light, never the Mission Director.

**Two Launches, and a $650M Programme Grant.** Launches carry two tonnes. Before the first Export can be sold, the Milestones need a landing pad (1240 kg, $30M) and an extraction plant, a vacuum fabrication plant and a second solar array to power them (1950 kg, $151M): two Launches. The first brings the landing pad with oxygen, water and spare Components ($200M with the Launch fee), and the second the export Plants ($301M). A full Return Mass of wafers sells for $150M, no more than the Launch fee, so wafers alone never pay for what a Launch brings: circuit boards, at twice the price, do. So the Launch after the first sale has to bring a circuit board plant, with the water-processing plant the water Milestone needs and supplies (about $265M), and the grant has to pay for what the sale doesn't. That comes to about $615M, so the Programme Grant is $650M. It couldn't go back to $400M without cutting the Launch fee to about $45M (and the wafer price with it), or the export Plants' prices by about two thirds. The cost is that no two-tonne Launch can cost more than the Programme Grant (the dearest comes to about $540M), so the Funding first runs short only after the second Launch; the Milestone hint for the first Launch says to keep enough for the export Plants on the next.

**The Radiation Dose.** Regolith shielding cuts the dose rate to a twentieth, 1.5 mSv an Earth Day. With Solar Particle Events on top it rises about 2 mSv an Earth Day, so the 600 mSv limit comes in about nine Lunations: the Hazard still bites over the long run. Water-wall shielding cuts it to a tenth of that again, 0.15 mSv an Earth Day. In the reference Campaign the Research Tasks pay for it by Earth Day 20, the third Launch brings its bladders, and it is filled once there is 300 kg of water to spare beyond a fortnight's drinking water, on Earth Day 96.0. From then on the limit is over four years away; without it, under Regolith shielding alone, it would come about five months after the end. The Radiation Dose never falls, so what that first Lunation or two takes is taken for good, and the water wall decides whether there is any of the limit left by the time a Buried Habitat can be had. A base that skips the wall reaches the limit around Earth Day 260, well before the around Earth Day 335 that it could afford a Buried Habitat even if it bought nothing else; a base behind the wall has about 1,400 Earth Days more to save up.

**Research that lasts.** The Research Tasks used to take 63 Earth Days back to back and pay 631 Science, against a Tech Tree costing 625, so a player who kept one running had every Design by about Earth Day 80, half-way through the reference Campaign. Twelve longer tasks, from 8 Earth Days to two Lunations and paying about 0.3 Science an hour (the earlier ones pay about 0.4), brought that to about 315 Earth Days and 2435 Science at the time. Seven of them are offered once a Design that had none of its own is unlocked. The Designs' costs now rise with their depth in the tree. Those the early game leans on cost what they did (Robots, Prefabricated and Dust-sealed solar arrays, Large battery banks and Water-wall shielding) or a little more, a few Earth Days of research each. The rest of the first rank cost 60–150, and those past it, at the time, 150–340, from a few weeks to a Lunation and a half of research each. The whole tree then cost 2225: the Research Tasks have to pay for it even when the last Design unlocked is Integrated fabrication, whose own task's 148 Science then has nothing left to buy. The reference Campaign unlocks only Water-wall shielding, at the same 40 Science and the same time, so its timeline is unchanged.

**The telescope.** Optical telescopes, a first-rank Industry Design at 60 Science that leads to nothing else, brings a telescope ($25M, 260 kg) that works on the observing programme, a standing Research Task, only at Night. Each round is 48 hours of one telescope's observing and earns 6 Science, so through a Night's 228 hours one telescope earns about 28 Science a Lunation: about an eighth of what Research Tasks run back to back pay, so it adds to research rather than replacing it, and pays back its Design in about two Lunations. It draws 0.25 kW only while it observes, 57 kWh through a whole Night, about half of what the starting battery banks have to spare once the Habitat and the heaters have theirs. Like a light, it stops observing rather than eat into the reserve, so a player who builds one has a reason to keep power in hand through the Night. Its Science is not counted in the balance between the Research Tasks and the Tech Tree above, and the reference Campaign builds none, so its timeline is unchanged.

**The Workshop.** The long-game base, with its 13 Structures, used 16.5 spare Components a Lunation between Earth Days 100 and 1,650 (from 8 to 25), as the long-game play guide showed before there was a Workshop. Metals piled up beside its circuit board plant at about 78 kg a Lunation. A Plant's Components come in whole numbers, so a Batch of a game-hour would make at least one an hour: 480 in a Daylight, about thirty times the use, and all the metals gone in days. So a Workshop's recipe has a Batch of its own length, a whole Earth Day (`batchHours`), and makes one spare Component in it: 20 in a Daylight, a little more than the base uses, leaving room for the Workshop's own wear (about one spare Component a Lunation) and the worse Lunations. Each takes 3.6 kg of metals, 72 kg a Lunation running through the Daylight: most of what the base has to spare, so that Act 2's Export of metals has to choose between selling them and running a Workshop. Against a Launch's spare Components, 20 kg and $1M each, it saves the base up to 400 kg of Payload Mass and $20M a Lunation, and its own 600 kg pays back in about a Lunation and a half; `npm run pacing-report` works this out. It costs $1000M, repriced from its first $40M for Act 2 (see Act 2: Funding below), which those spares alone would never pay back: what does is the Launch fee saved once a Launch brings nothing, about $170M a Lunation. It draws 2 kW, which the long-game base's two solar arrays have to spare beside its other Plants at 13 kW, except for short spells when Dust and wear have cut them. Its Design, Parts machining, costs 150 Science, in the second rank of the tree past Molten regolith electrolysis. So that the Research Tasks still pay for the whole tree even when Integrated fabrication is the last Design unlocked, the Machined part wear trial pays 108 Science of it, over 14 Earth Days: at the time that made them 2543 Science against the Designs' 2375, a margin of 20 once Integrated fabrication's own task has nothing left to buy. The reference Campaign never unlocks it, so its timeline is unchanged. One Workshop was enough for the long-game base as it stood, but not once Act 2 had grown it: see below.

**The Buried Habitat.** ADR-0008 chose it as the way past the Radiation Dose limit. Behind a water wall the dose rises 0.15 mSv an Earth Day and, with Solar Particle Events, 0.197 in the long-game play guide: they multiply it 1.32 times, against the 1.33 that their average length and spacing work out at. So that 30 mSv lasts 1,400 Earth Days, the rate with Solar Particle Events has to be at most 0.02, so the usual rate is 0.015, a tenth of a water wall's. In the long-game play guide it came to 0.0196 mSv an Earth Day from moving in on Earth Day 1,593 at 558.0 mSv to Earth Day 3,000 at 585.5 mSv, with the game's forecast then putting the limit on Earth Day 3,964. A base that doesn't build one is unchanged: the same rates, and the same limit, put at about Earth Day 1,790 at the time (see [Targets](#targets) for what has been measured). Its eight vault sections and deep life support core cost $4,450M and weigh 4,900 kg, and with a Habitat's $60M, 400 kg airlock come to three Launches' worth, $450M of fees: $4,960M in all, as the long-game play guide paid (Launches on Earth Days 1,537, 1,548 and 1,569). A section at $500M is the most any Launch item can cost, so that a Launch with one in it could still be paid for from the Programme Grant, were there nothing else on it. That is about what 17 Launches of circuit boards sell for ($300M a Launch, with no fee on one that brings nothing), 255 Earth Days of what a base earns at a trip every 15.4 Earth Days, and about a quarter of the $18,080M the long-game base held on Earth Day 1,530, even after Act 2's purchases. It held $22,400M before them, and Act 3 is to bring more to buy. It takes 120 hours of the excavator's Site Preparation, against no more than 9 for anything else: the 5 Earth Days and about half the excavator's Condition keep it from being built in a moment without leaving the excavator Broken part-way. Its Design costs 400 Science. The Buried vault dose survey pays that, over two Lunations, needing Water-wall shielding: at the time that made the Research Tasks 2943 Science against the Designs' 2775, the same margin of 20 once Integrated fabrication's own task has nothing left to buy, and 389.5 Earth Days back to back. The reference Campaign never gets that far, so its timeline is unchanged. Moving out of a water-walled Habitat leaves it drawing 1.2 kW, and on the long-game base that is too much to keep: its battery banks can't carry both Habitats through a Night, and a first attempt that added a large battery bank and kept both fell to no spare Components by Earth Day 1,700 and died on Earth Day 1,789 once the solar arrays wore out, as the Workshop is last in line for power. So the guide builds the Buried Habitat early in a Daylight, and has the old Habitat, its water wall and its shielding dismantled before Night falls. That is a choice for a player too, not a rule.

**Act 2: what limits each Export.** A circuit board plant makes 192 kg in a Daylight against the 295 kg that a round trip every 10 Earth Days can carry away in a Lunation, and in the long game before Act 2 the Lander sold a full Return Mass of them twice a Lunation, waiting on the Plant each time. So a Plant's rate limits them. Metals had to hit something else, and it can't be the Plant: an extraction plant already makes 288 kg a Daylight, for $75M. So they hit the Return Mass. At $1 a kilogram a full Return Mass sells for $100M, under the Launch fee, and they only earn by filling what circuit boards leave: about 36 kg a trip, once the Lander goes every round trip rather than waits. Any dearer and an extraction plant alone would out-earn a vacuum fabrication plant by too much, and Act 1 would skip the wafers. In Earth Days 350 to 900 the long-game play guide sold 3,534 kg of circuit boards and 1,566 kg of metals over 51 trips, $12,168M, of which metals were 13%. That brings the base up against the Return Mass, which is what Act 3 opens with. A magnetic separator and a bucket-wheel digger were tried on paper, to make Regolith the limit, and cut: a second extraction plant out-made the separator at a twelfth of its price, so neither would have changed a decision.

**Act 2: Science.** Nine Research Tasks add 495.5 Earth Days, and 3,095 Science, paying less for each hour the longer they run, down to about 0.25. None can be started in the reference Campaign: each needs a Design it never unlocks, or runs longer than the longest it gets to. Four Designs cost 500, 600, 800 and 1,050 Science, so at the time the Research Tasks paid 6,038 against the Designs' 5,725, a margin of 68 once Cold-trap mining's own task has nothing left to buy. Played, the last Design is unlocked on Earth Day 864, and the last Research Task completed on 901.

**Act 2: Funding.** Everything the reference Campaign buys has to stay as cheap as it is, or its timeline changes, so all Funding can do is come to matter in what is bought later. That is a Workshop, repriced from $40M to $1000M; an array works at $1500M; a concentrator tower at $2000M; a thermal store at $2000M; and a cold-trap ice line at $3000M: $9,500M, 70% of the $13,620M the pacing report has Exports earning over the Act, with one of every Launch item now taking 42 Launches' profit. No item costs more than $500M. In the long-game play guide, which buys two Workshops and leaves out the thermal store, the Funding stays around $1,000M from Earth Day 400 to 600, with a low of $460M on Earth Day 572. It is then Science that decides when the next purchase comes, and the Funding climbs: $3,084M on Earth Day 700, $4,120M on 900, $18,080M on 1,530, where it was $22,407M before Act 2. So Funding bites for about the first half of the Act, and no further. The long game also ends on Earth Day 3,000 with more than it did, $45,418M against $42,625M, as the extra trips earn more than Act 2 costs: the rest of the Campaign needs Acts 3 and 4 to give it something to spend on.

**Act 2: the Designs.** Each changes a decision. Array fabrication: spend silicon and metals on power, or sell them and pay for a Launch. Concentrator towers: ten cheap arrays with ten visits a Lunation and over 15 spare Components, or one dear tower. Thermal storage: run Plants through the Night on a row of large battery banks (four Launches, heaters and wear) or on one dear store. Cold-trap mining: keep sending a Robot off the map for ice, for free, or pay never to. The long-game play guide skips the thermal store, as running the circuit board chain through the Night would fill the Return Mass with boards and leave no room for metals; the rule `design-has-a-purpose` passes it as another way to store power. The guide needs a second Workshop: with only one, 20 spare Components a Lunation fell short once the base had grown, it ran out by Earth Day 530 and its solar arrays wearing out unrepaired killed the Mission Director on Earth Day 935. With two it never fell below 18 after the first was commissioned, and stands at about 38.

**Act 3: propellant.** Propellant is liquid oxygen, a bulk resource of its own rather than the Mission Director's oxygen, so that Export and the consistency rules didn't have to change and selling it can't suffocate anyone. The Lander carries 100 kg on what Earth gives it, and a kilogram more for every 2 kg burned, up to 400 kg. So 2 kg of propellant, worth $2.40M sold, must lift more than that: circuit boards at $3M a kilogram are worth it, metals at $1M aren't. That is the Act's choice, and at $1.20M propellant takes metals' place in what the boards leave of the first 100 kg. A propellant plant makes 480 kg in a Daylight for 15 kg of Regolith an hour; a large one, 1,440 kg for 35 kg an hour, which keeps the excavator busy. In the long-game play guide, one was enough to fly 300 kg once, on Earth Day 1,014, 21 days after the first propellant.

**Act 4: precision optics.** They sell for $6M a kilogram, twice circuit boards. An optics works makes 96 kg in a Daylight, a grinding line 240 kg. At $9M the guide ended with $118,875M. At $6M it still ended with $91,680M, before they took water: see below.

**Act 4: water as coolant.** An optics works takes 1 kg of the Mission Director's water an hour and a grinding line 2.5 kg, 5 kg for each kilogram of optics, so that water ties into the main late-game income. The playtest (#189) kept these numbers. Together they want 1,680 kg in a Daylight, against the 432 kg one water-processing plant can make, and the 173 kg one cold-trap ice line's ice comes to; the Mission Director drinks 103 kg a Lunation. The reserve is a fortnight of what they drink, 49 kg, in the data catalogue for each stock (`reserveEarthDays`), the same as a water wall leaves. It has to outlast a Night, 9.5 Earth Days, as Plants that make water may stand idle through one, and a Lander's round trip, 10; and it has to be more than the first warning, 7 Earth Days, so that Plants taking what is above it give no warning. What Plants use doesn't count against self-sufficiency in water (see [Self-sufficiency](rules.md#self-sufficiency)): counting it would call a base not self-sufficient whose Mission Director is in no danger.

The long-game play guide, at data catalogue version 67, plays no differently for it: it still builds the same one water-processing plant and cold-trap ice line, and brings 100 kg of water by Launch, at $154M with the fee, whenever it has under 120 kg. It completes with every pacing target met, and nothing in Acts 1 to 3 moves: the Funding is still $14,524M on Earth Day 1,500, precision optics are first sold on 1,688, the last Design is unlocked on 2,381 and research ends on 2,981. In Act 4 it sold 2,349 kg of precision optics, 10,361 kg of circuit boards and 3,610 kg of propellant, $49,509M over 153 trips, and ends with $42,805M against the $91,680M it had before. 65 of the 126 Launches after the first optics sold were sent, at $154M, with water and nothing else of note. Its water reaches its lowest, 14.8 kg, on Earth Day 2,908.7: the Plants stop at 49 kg at dusk, and the Mission Director drinks 33 kg of that through the Night, which leaves about 4 Earth Days of use at dawn. No warning comes, as the forecast at Night looks only as far as the next Daylight, and the stock lasts it. The Mission Director moved into the Buried Habitat on Earth Day 1,591.4 at 561.7 mSv and ended at 588.9 mSv, 0.0194 mSv an Earth Day, with the limit forecast for Earth Day 3,738.

**Storage limits.** The base holds 400 kg of water and 100 kg of oxygen with no tank (`storageLimitKilograms` in the data catalogue). Water's has to hold the first water wall's 300 kg and its 49 kg reserve, even a batch below the limit, where a Plant rests: 399 kg does, with 50 kg to spare. Oxygen's is about four months of what the Mission Director breathes, and over twice the most the reference Campaign keeps before it turns its extraction plant back to metals. Neither is meant to bite in Act 1 or Act 2, and what lets a base past them is tanks (see below). A Plant that makes only a full stock starts no batch that wouldn't fit, rather than one whenever there is any room: otherwise it would take a whole hour's ice for the few grams drunk since the last, and lose nearly all of it, which is the waste the limit is there to save.

Waiting for tank space counts towards self-sufficiency as if the Plant had made what was used, up to what it makes when it runs, Night included, as otherwise a base would lose the title and the Milestones for having too much. It is a little generous: a Plant that makes less than is used over a Lunation, sitting behind a store filled by Launches, is credited while it waits. That lasts only until the store falls by a batch, or a Launch fills it again at the Launch's price. So the excess of a landing is lost, not kept: it is the simple rule, it is warned of, and keeping it would need somewhere to keep it, which is what tanks are for.

The reference Campaign's timeline is identical at data catalogue version 68: every date, Launch and sale. So is the long-game play guide's until Earth Day 1,100, well into Act 3, with Funding still $4,120M on Earth Day 900. From there its trips start to differ by fractions of a day; which Plant first waited there hasn't been traced. Every pacing target is still met. The last Design is unlocked on 2,380.8 and research ends on 2,980.8, as before. The Mission Director moves into a Buried Habitat on 1,590.4 (from 1,591.4), first precision optics are sold on 1,701.5 (from 1,688.0), the Funding on Earth Day 1,500 is $14,676M (from $14,524M) and at the end $43,178M (from $42,805M). In Act 4 it sold 2,409 kg of precision optics (from 2,349), 10,294 kg of circuit boards (from 10,361) and 3,592 kg of propellant (from 3,610). Its water's lowest is still 14.8 kg, now on Earth Day 2,082.8, and its oxygen's lowest is unchanged.

**Storage tanks.** A water tank adds 1,000 kg to the limit, for 220 kg and $15M shipped, and an oxygen tank adds 300 kg, for 400 kg and $35M: one Launch brings both, with room to spare. 1,000 kg is about what a Greenhouse's Lunation (#186) or a Daylight of a grinding line asks for, and 300 kg of oxygen is about a year of what the Mission Director breathes, so one of each is a real step and more can follow. The prices are low against what a base earns by the time it wants them: they are about the Payload Mass and the power, not the money. They replace the liquid oxygen tank and the bulk water tank, Launch items that couldn't fit the limits from the ticket before.

A water tank's heater draws 0.05 kW at Night, 11 kWh through one, about a twentieth of a battery bank; an oxygen tank's cooler 0.15 kW, 34 kWh, about a sixth. So one tank never asks for a battery bank, and a tank farm does. With no power at Night a water tank loses 2% of what it holds an Earth Day, a sixth of it through a Night, and an oxygen tank 10%, about three fifths: enough to matter to a base that cables a tank to a solar array alone, and not so much that a bad Night empties the store before the warning is read. The rule keeps the Mission Director's reserve, like a Plant's water, so that no failure of a tank can kill them. A Broken tank's share goes at once, as a Broken battery bank's energy does, and the reserve keeps that from killing them as well. Dismantling, being a choice, loses only what no longer fits. Their Condition doesn't cut what they hold before they are Broken: a limit that wobbled with wear would lose stock a little at a time for no decision.

Bulk supply tanks stays at 180 Science, needing Life support resupply and Water electrolysis. Looked at again for what it now does: it comes a good while before a base needs it, as neither limit bites until Act 3 or 4, so it could stand to be dearer or deeper. **What the limit does for #176: not much.** The question was whether surplus makes burning propellant free. In the guide it does, and the limit doesn't change that, as the limit caps the stock and the surplus is in the flow. Its two Plants could make 1,920 kg in a Daylight. It does three smaller things, none of which makes the choice real for a rich base:

- **On one trip, up to 1,000 kg, burning and selling compete.** What a load takes is 2 kg for each kilogram over 100 kg, plus what is sold. With 450 kg, a load of 300 kg can hold only 50 kg of propellant, and each kilogram sold above that gives up half a kilogram of lift. That is worth $1.50M in circuit boards against the $1.20M it sells for, which is the choice #176 asks about, but it lasts only until $80M buys a tank.
- **A large Plant meets the limit; a small one hardly does.** A small Plant takes 19 Earth Days of Daylight to fill 450 kg, and makes 240 kg in a round trip at best, so it runs short before it runs full. A large one fills it in 6, so that with 720 kg a round trip it could burn more than it can keep. A tank then pays for itself in one trip: 150 kg more burned lifts 75 kg more of circuit boards, $225M, if a base has them, which the guide doesn't.
- **Surplus stops piling up.** A Plant at the limit waits, using no Regolith, so a base no longer has to switch it off to keep the excavator free, and has no great hoard to live on through bad Lunations.

So if burning should cost a rich base something, that has to come from somewhere else: a dearer or slower Plant, or more than the Return Mass has room for. That stays with #176's playtest.

**A Plant that stops counting.** A Broken propellant plant stops counting, by the tank's rule, and starts again when it is repaired, but unlike a tank **its share isn't lost**: the limit falls and the stock stays, over it if need be, as an over-limit stock always does. The same goes for one the Robot has started taking apart, all of it, where a tank would lose what no longer fits. A Plant isn't a choice of where to keep things, as a tank is. A base has to have one to have any propellant, and wears it about five to eight times as fast as a tank, so taking its share would have a stray Micrometeorite empty the store of a base with one Plant, for nothing the player could have done but build a tank. And a base with no working Plant and no tank has **a limit of none**, not a refusal: it keeps what it has, can burn and sell it, and can't make more, having nothing to make it with. The status panel then shows the amount alone. A tank on its own still holds what it can, and what it holds is still at risk.

**Tanks on a bulk resource.** Nobody depends on propellant, so no reserve is kept: a Broken tank's share is a real loss, all of the stock if the tank held it all, and it costs up to $720M of sales. One lost power loses 10% of its share an Earth Day, as oxygen does, the same liquid. ## What the first Acts taught

Issue #158's lessons. The maintainer played Acts 1 and 2 and confirms they are fun; everything else here comes from the pacing report and three runs of the long-game play guide, so it says what the numbers do, not how it feels. No number in Act 1 or Act 2 changed: all their pacing targets were met, and they play well.

- **Funding bites for less than half of the Campaign.** It stays about $1,000M to Earth Day 600, as Act 2 means it to, then climbs for good: $4,120M on Earth Day 900, $14,524M on 1,500 and $91,680M on 3,000 (now $43,178M, as precision optics take water; see [Act 4: water as coolant](#the-larger-changes-and-why)). Lowering the optics' price only slows that. Acts 3 and 4 add $5,700M to buy, against tens of billions earned, and nothing later in the Campaign asks for Funding. It needs something to spend it on at scale, which is a design question rather than a price (#179).
- **Payload Mass isn't a limit after Act 2.** A Launch carries every Structure's Components but a Buried Habitat's, and a base self-sufficient in spares sends most Launches empty, at no fee.
- **Power stops being a limit at the first concentrator tower.** Its 100 kW covers everything the Campaign ever builds. Its 0.2 kW of steady draw is what matters: on a base with the starting battery banks a second and third tower killed the Mission Director at the end of a Night on Earth Day 1,255. The forecast warned, but nothing says why, or that towers beyond the first add nothing.
- **Research is what paces the late game.** With Funding and power to spare, the Design dates set when each Act's Plants arrive, and from Earth Day 2,381 there is nothing left to unlock.
- **The play guide is an expert.** It had a Workshop running on Earth Day 372 against a target of 500. A target is a limit for a slower player, so the guide meeting it early doesn't mean it is wrong.
- **What only a person can tell.** When this was written, Acts 3 and 4 had been played only by the guide (the maintainer has since played them for the water economy, #189), which switches its propellant plants off above 1,200 kg. A large plant makes more than a base can sell, so surplus may make burning free, and the choice obvious (#176; [the propellant limit](#the-larger-changes-and-why) changes that little). The last Research Task, 600 Earth Days long, pays Science with nothing left to buy, so from Earth Day 2,381 the game asks little (#179). That a Plant rather than the Lander limits income after Act 3 is what the pacing report works out; no target checks it in play.

## Still to watch

- **Funding in the late game.** See above. It is the Campaign's largest open balance problem. The Greenhouse chain takes $28,053M of it in the guide, and leaves it idle again by the end (see [The Greenhouse chain](#the-larger-changes-and-why)).
- **What Science buys.** The playtest (#189) kept the chain's numbers. A player with several Greenhouses early reaches Vault cistern on about Earth Day 2,200, with 800 Earth Days to spare.

- **A second circuit board chain.** A second extraction plant, vacuum fabrication plant and circuit board plant cost about $200M and fill the Return Mass with circuit boards, worth three times metals. They are the reference Campaign's items, so their prices can't rise without changing its timeline, and it stays the best money a base can spend, far better than anything in Act 2. Metals then have no room, and Act 3's propellant isn't worth burning for them.

- **Two Habitats on one network.** Commissioning a Buried Habitat beside the old one adds 1.2 kW, which a two-array base can't spare: its forecast warns of the Night, and the Workshop, among the last in line for power, runs less. Dismantling the old one, or adding solar arrays and battery banks, cures it, but nothing but the forecast tells the player so.
- **Machine heaters.** The Machines' and Rover's heaters draw from the Habitat's network every Night, because the Robot parks them beside the nearest powered Structure. That is about a third of what the battery banks have to carry, and nothing tells the player. It is how the code parks them, so it is noted here rather than balanced away.
- **Spare Components.** Dust wears everything down steadily, so a growing base uses spare Components fast: the reference Campaign keeps 30 in stock, topped up by every Launch, until a Workshop takes that over. With fewer, Maintenance waits for spares, solar arrays wear down to Broken, and a Night can then empty the battery banks. With one Workshop the long-game play guide's stock fell to 3 on about Earth Day 2,685 and recovered, for a cause never found; with two and a concentrator tower it no longer does, and its low after Earth Day 2,500 was 25. It has since been less comfortable: with a Greenhouse's second Robot and more Structures its low was 12 at data catalogue version 70, and 2 at version 71, on about Earth Day 2,980, with no trace yet of why. At version 72 it has a third Workshop: with two, a growing hall and two ice lines, in a run also short of water, it stayed at about none from Earth Day 2,330. With three, and all the chain's Structures, its low was 7.
- **One extraction plant.** It can make oxygen or silicon, but not both at once, so wafer and circuit board production slows while the oxygen is topped up.
- **Water for precision optics.** A Launch's 100 kg of water, at $154M with the fee, cools only 20 kg of precision optics, worth $120M, so a guide that kept its optics going on that lost money on the water alone. It now makes its water at the base for its Greenhouse, and its optics run on what is over (see [The Greenhouse](#the-larger-changes-and-why)). The playtest (#189) kept what water and ice pay.
- **Water.** The water wall takes 300 kg; after filling it the reference Campaign's water fell to 31 kg (under nine Earth Days) before the next Expedition's ice came through. A player should send the Rover again as soon as the wall is filled.
- **The first Launch's Funding.** With the Programme Grant above what any one Launch can cost, nothing stops a player spending most of it on the first Launch but the hint; one who does can't afford the export Plants, and has no way to earn more.

## The catalogue's consistency rules

`test/catalogue-rules.test.ts` holds the data catalogue to these rules with the other tests, so that a bad addition fails with a clear reason, naming the item and the rule, and nobody has to replay the game to find it:

- **`design-has-a-purpose`:** every Design brings something the long-game play guide uses (`designsTheLongGameUses`), or something from another Design does the same job, so that it offers a choice.
- **`design-not-beaten`:** no Design is beaten outright by something at least as cheap, in Science on the way to it, money and mass, and at least as good at the same job, as the pacing report works it out.
- **`export-can-be-sold`:** every Export can be made by some Plant, and sold within the Return Mass.
- **`bulk-resource-has-a-use`:** every bulk resource goes into some Plant, or is an Export.
- **`research-fits-the-campaign`:** the Research Tasks, back to back from about Earth Day 16, when the guide starts its first, finish by the long game's end on Earth Day 3,000. The guide fails if any is left undone, which only a whole play would otherwise show. `test/reference-campaign.test.ts` checks that first start.

An item may break a rule only if it is on the exemption list in `test/catalogue-rules.ts`, with the reason. An exemption for something that no longer breaks the rule fails too, so the list can't go stale.
