# Balancing

The data catalogue's numbers are balanced against the reference Campaign's timeline, so that a new player can play from the landing to a self-sufficient, export-funded base by following the Milestones, and carry on afterwards. Every balancing change is in the data catalogue; none is in code.

The [reference Campaign](reference-campaign.md) and its commands are how these numbers are measured.

## Targets

The spec's tunable defaults all hold:

- 1x is 6 game-minutes a second.
- A Lunation is 20 Earth Days of Daylight and 9.5 of Night, and the first Night falls on Earth Day 8.
- The Lander's legs take 3, 4 and 3 Earth Days.
- The Robot repairs itself below 10% Condition.
- The starting oxygen and water last about 30 Earth Days.

The pacing targets, and where the reference Campaign came at data catalogue version 65:

| The Radiation Dose, played as the hints and Designs allow, survivable for years | Over 4 years from the end with a water wall; about 5 months under Regolith shielding alone |

The reference Campaign's test checks each of these.

## The long Campaign's Acts

The Campaign's content is to last about 10 real hours at 20x (3,000 Earth Days, as an hour at 20x is 300 Earth Days), in four Acts (ADR-0008). Acts 1 and 2 are built. Acts 3 and 4 are decided here but only Act 4's Buried Habitat is built yet, so their other numbers are targets for the tickets that build them. A Campaign stays open-ended: it doesn't stop on Earth Day 3,000, but its content has run out by then.

## The larger changes, and why

**Battery banks.** A Night lasts 228 hours. Through it the Habitat draws 274 kWh (1.2 kW), each solar array 11 kWh, and the heaters of the excavator, the regolith printer and the Rover, which the Robot plugs in before every Night, 137 kWh between them. A battery bank stores 200 kWh, about what a small electric car carries, and its heaters draw 0.07 kW, 16 kWh through a Night. The starting stock holds three battery banks, 600 kWh, which carry the Habitat, two solar arrays and all the heaters through a Night, about 480 kWh, with over 100 kWh to spare; two would not. The large battery bank stays the better buy: 800 kWh for 0.2 kW of heaters, less mass and money for each kWh. (A battery bank used to store 60 kWh, and its heaters to draw 0.25 kW, 57 of those 60 through a Night, so no number of battery banks could carry anything else through one.)

The Habitat's first Night has to be carried by the starting stock. The Lander can't leave until the Mission Director has moved in, and a round trip takes 10 Earth Days. Large battery banks need Science, and the Milestones put the first Research Task after the landing pad, which the first Launch brings, so they could arrive at the earliest on the second Launch, as the Night falls and uncharged. Staying aboard the Lander through the first Night only puts the problem off to the second. The first two Milestone hints say to build all the starting battery banks and to move in once they are charged, so a player following them gets through the first Night. The starting stock's four Light Masts would draw over 360 kWh through it, more than is left once the Habitat and the heaters have theirs, so the lights switch themselves off part-way through rather than leave the Habitat without power: building them costs a new player some light, never the Mission Director.

**Two Launches, and a $650M Programme Grant.** Launches carry two tonnes. Before the first Export can be sold, the Milestones need a landing pad (1240 kg, $30M) and an extraction plant, a vacuum fabrication plant and a second solar array to power them (1950 kg, $151M): two Launches. The first brings the landing pad with oxygen, water and spare Components ($200M with the Launch fee), and the second the export Plants ($301M). A full Return Mass of wafers sells for $150M, no more than the Launch fee, so wafers alone never pay for what a Launch brings: circuit boards, at twice the price, do. So the Launch after the first sale has to bring a circuit board plant, with the water-processing plant the water Milestone needs and supplies (about $265M), and the grant has to pay for what the sale doesn't. That comes to about $615M, so the Programme Grant is $650M. It couldn't go back to $400M without cutting the Launch fee to about $45M (and the wafer price with it), or the export Plants' prices by about two thirds. The cost is that no two-tonne Launch can cost more than the Programme Grant (the dearest comes to about $540M), so the Funding first runs short only after the second Launch; the Milestone hint for the first Launch says to keep enough for the export Plants on the next.

**The Radiation Dose.** Regolith shielding cuts the dose rate to a twentieth, 1.5 mSv an Earth Day. With Solar Particle Events on top it rises about 2 mSv an Earth Day, so the 600 mSv limit comes in about nine Lunations: the Hazard still bites over the long run. Water-wall shielding cuts it to a tenth of that again, 0.15 mSv an Earth Day. In the reference Campaign the Research Tasks pay for it by Earth Day 20, the third Launch brings its bladders, and it is filled once there is 300 kg of water to spare beyond a fortnight's drinking water, on Earth Day 96.0. From then on the limit is over four years away; without it, under Regolith shielding alone, it would come about five months after the end. The Radiation Dose never falls, so what that first Lunation or two takes is taken for good, and the water wall decides whether there is any of the limit left by the time a Buried Habitat can be had. A base that skips the wall reaches the limit around Earth Day 260, well before the around Earth Day 335 that it could afford a Buried Habitat even if it bought nothing else; a base behind the wall has about 2,500 Earth Days to save up.

**Research that lasts.** The Research Tasks used to take 63 Earth Days back to back and pay 631 Science, against a Tech Tree costing 625, so a player who kept one running had every Design by about Earth Day 80, half-way through the reference Campaign. Twelve longer tasks, from 8 Earth Days to two Lunations and paying about 0.3 Science an hour (the earlier ones pay about 0.4), bring that to about 315 Earth Days and 2435 Science. Seven of them are offered once a Design that had none of its own is unlocked. The Designs' costs now rise with their depth in the tree. Those the early game leans on cost what they did (Robots, Prefabricated and Dust-sealed solar arrays, Large battery banks and Water-wall shielding) or a little more, a few Earth Days of research each. The rest of the first rank cost 60–150, and those past it 150–340, from a few weeks to a Lunation and a half of research each. The whole tree costs 2225: the Research Tasks have to pay that even when the last Design unlocked is Integrated fabrication, whose own task's 148 Science then has nothing left to buy. The reference Campaign unlocks only Water-wall shielding, at the same 40 Science and the same time, so its timeline is unchanged.

**The telescope.** Optical telescopes, a first-rank Industry Design at 60 Science that leads to nothing else, brings a telescope ($25M, 260 kg) that works on the observing programme, a standing Research Task, only at Night. Each round is 48 hours of one telescope's observing and earns 6 Science, so through a Night's 228 hours one telescope earns about 28 Science a Lunation: about an eighth of what Research Tasks run back to back pay, so it adds to research rather than replacing it, and pays back its Design in about two Lunations. It draws 0.25 kW only while it observes, 57 kWh through a whole Night, about half of what the starting battery banks have to spare once the Habitat and the heaters have theirs. Like a light, it stops observing rather than eat into the reserve, so a player who builds one has a reason to keep power in hand through the Night. Its Science is not counted in the balance between the Research Tasks and the Tech Tree above, and the reference Campaign builds none, so its timeline is unchanged.

**The Workshop.** The long-game base, with its 13 Structures, used 16.5 spare Components a Lunation between Earth Days 100 and 1,650 (from 8 to 25), as the long-game play guide showed before there was a Workshop. Metals piled up beside its circuit board plant at about 78 kg a Lunation. A Plant's Components come in whole numbers, so a Batch of a game-hour would make at least one an hour: 480 in a Daylight, about thirty times the use, and all the metals gone in days. So a Workshop's recipe has a Batch of its own length, a whole Earth Day (`batchHours`), and makes one spare Component in it: 20 in a Daylight, a little more than the base uses, leaving room for the Workshop's own wear (about one spare Component a Lunation) and the worse Lunations. Each takes 3.6 kg of metals, 72 kg a Lunation running through the Daylight: most of what the base has to spare, so that Act 2's Export of metals has to choose between selling them and running a Workshop. Against a Launch's spare Components, 20 kg and $1M each, it saves the base up to 400 kg of Payload Mass and $20M a Lunation, and its own 600 kg pays back in about a Lunation and a half; `npm run pacing-report` works this out. It costs $1000M (see Act 2 below), which those spares alone would never pay back: what does is the Launch fee saved once a Launch brings nothing, about $170M a Lunation. It draws 2 kW, which the long-game base's two solar arrays have to spare beside its other Plants at 13 kW, except for short spells when Dust and wear have cut them. Its Design, Parts machining, costs 150 Science, in the second rank of the tree past Molten regolith electrolysis. So that the Research Tasks still pay for the whole tree even when Integrated fabrication is the last Design unlocked, the Machined part wear trial pays 108 Science of it, over 14 Earth Days: they now pay 2543 Science against the Designs' 2375, a margin of 20 once Integrated fabrication's own task has nothing left to buy. The reference Campaign never unlocks it, so its timeline is unchanged. One Workshop was enough for the long-game base as it stood, but not once Act 2 had grown it: see below.

**The Buried Habitat.** ADR-0008 chose it as the way past the Radiation Dose limit. Behind a water wall the dose rises 0.15 mSv an Earth Day and, with Solar Particle Events, 0.197 in the long-game play guide: they multiply it 1.32 times, against the 1.33 that their average length and spacing work out at. So that 30 mSv lasts 1,400 Earth Days, the rate with Solar Particle Events has to be at most 0.02, so the usual rate is 0.015, a tenth of a water wall's. In the long-game play guide it came to 0.0196 mSv an Earth Day from moving in on Earth Day 1,593 at 558.0 mSv to Earth Day 3,000 at 585.5 mSv, with the game's forecast then putting the limit on Earth Day 3,964. A base that doesn't build one is unchanged: the same rates, and the limit about Earth Day 1,790. Its eight vault sections and deep life support core cost $4,450M and weigh 4,900 kg, and with a Habitat's $60M, 400 kg airlock come to three Launches' worth, $450M of fees: $4,960M in all, as the long-game play guide paid (Launches on Earth Days 1,537, 1,548 and 1,569). A section at $500M is the most any Launch item can cost, so that a Launch with one in it could still be paid for from the Programme Grant, were there nothing else on it. That is about what 17 Launches of circuit boards sell for ($300M a Launch, with no fee on one that brings nothing), 255 Earth Days of what a base earns at a trip every 15.4 Earth Days, and about a quarter of the $18,080M the long-game base held on Earth Day 1,530, even after Act 2's purchases. It held $22,400M before them, and Act 3 is to bring more to buy. It takes 120 hours of the excavator's Site Preparation, against no more than 9 for anything else: the 5 Earth Days and about half the excavator's Condition keep it from being built in a moment without leaving the excavator Broken part-way. Its Design costs 400 Science. The Buried vault dose survey pays that, over two Lunations, needing Water-wall shielding: the Research Tasks now pay 2943 Science against the Designs' 2775, the same margin of 20 once Integrated fabrication's own task has nothing left to buy, and take 389.5 Earth Days back to back. The reference Campaign never gets that far, so its timeline is unchanged. Moving out of a water-walled Habitat leaves it drawing 1.2 kW, and on the long-game base that is too much to keep: its battery banks can't carry both Habitats through a Night, and a first attempt that added a large battery bank and kept both fell to no spare Components by Earth Day 1,700 and died on Earth Day 1,789 once the solar arrays wore out, as the Workshop is last in line for power. So the guide builds the Buried Habitat early in a Daylight, and has the old Habitat, its water wall and its shielding dismantled before Night falls. That is a choice for a player too, not a rule.

**Act 2: what limits each Export.** A circuit board plant makes 192 kg in a Daylight against the 295 kg that a round trip every 10 Earth Days can carry away in a Lunation, and in the long game before Act 2 the Lander sold a full Return Mass of them twice a Lunation, waiting on the Plant each time. So a Plant's rate limits them. Metals had to hit something else, and it can't be the Plant: an extraction plant already makes 288 kg a Daylight, for $75M. So they hit the Return Mass. At $1 a kilogram a full Return Mass sells for $100M, under the Launch fee, and they only earn by filling what circuit boards leave: about 36 kg a trip, once the Lander goes every round trip rather than waits. Any dearer and an extraction plant alone would out-earn a vacuum fabrication plant by too much, and Act 1 would skip the wafers. In Earth Days 350 to 900 the long-game play guide sold 3,534 kg of circuit boards and 1,566 kg of metals over 51 trips, $12,168M, of which metals were 13%. That brings the base up against the Return Mass, which is what Act 3 opens with. A magnetic separator and a bucket-wheel digger were tried on paper, to make Regolith the limit, and cut: a second extraction plant out-made the separator at a twelfth of its price, so neither would have changed a decision.

**Act 2: Science.** Nine Research Tasks add 495.5 Earth Days, and 3,095 Science, paying less for each hour the longer they run, down to about 0.25. None can be started in the reference Campaign: each needs a Design it never unlocks, or runs longer than the longest it gets to. Four Designs cost 500, 600, 800 and 1,050 Science, so the Research Tasks pay 6,038 against the Designs' 5,725, a margin of 68 once Cold-trap mining's own task has nothing left to buy. Played, the last Design is unlocked on Earth Day 864, and the last Research Task completed on 901.

**Act 2: Funding.** Everything the reference Campaign buys has to stay as cheap as it is, or its timeline changes, so all Funding can do is come to matter in what is bought later. That is a Workshop, repriced from $40M to $1000M; an array works at $1500M; a concentrator tower at $2000M; a thermal store at $2000M; and a cold-trap ice line at $3000M: $9,500M, 70% of the $13,620M the pacing report has Exports earning over the Act, with one of every Launch item now taking 42 Launches' profit. No item costs more than $500M. In the long-game play guide, which buys two Workshops and leaves out the thermal store, the Funding stays around $1,000M from Earth Day 400 to 600, with a low of $460M on Earth Day 572. It is then Science that decides when the next purchase comes, and the Funding climbs: $3,084M on Earth Day 700, $4,120M on 900, $18,080M on 1,530, where it was $22,407M before Act 2. So Funding bites for about the first half of the Act, and no further. The long game also ends on Earth Day 3,000 with more than it did, $45,418M against $42,625M, as the extra trips earn more than Act 2 costs: the rest of the Campaign needs Acts 3 and 4 to give it something to spend on.

**Act 2: the Designs.** Each changes a decision. Array fabrication: spend silicon and metals on power, or sell them and pay for a Launch. Concentrator towers: ten cheap arrays with ten visits a Lunation and over 15 spare Components, or one dear tower. Thermal storage: run Plants through the Night on a row of large battery banks (four Launches, heaters and wear) or on one dear store. Cold-trap mining: keep sending a Robot off the map for ice, for free, or pay never to. The long-game play guide skips the thermal store, as running the circuit board chain through the Night would fill the Return Mass with boards and leave no room for metals; the rule `design-has-a-purpose` passes it as another way to store power. The guide needs a second Workshop: with only one, 20 spare Components a Lunation fell short once the base had grown, it ran out by Earth Day 530 and its solar arrays wearing out unrepaired killed the Mission Director on Earth Day 935. With two it never fell below 18 after the first was commissioned, and stands at about 38.

## Still to watch

- **A second circuit board chain.** A second extraction plant, vacuum fabrication plant and circuit board plant cost about $200M and fill the Return Mass with circuit boards, worth three times metals. They are the reference Campaign's items, so their prices can't rise without changing its timeline, and it stays the best money a base can spend, far better than anything in Act 2. Metals then have no room until Act 3 raises the Return Mass.

- **Two Habitats on one network.** Commissioning a Buried Habitat beside the old one adds 1.2 kW, which a two-array base can't spare: its forecast warns of the Night, and the Workshop, among the last in line for power, runs less. Dismantling the old one, or adding solar arrays and battery banks, cures it, but nothing but the forecast tells the player so.
- **Machine heaters.** The Machines' and Rover's heaters draw from the Habitat's network every Night, because the Robot parks them beside the nearest powered Structure. That is about a third of what the battery banks have to carry, and nothing tells the player. It is how the code parks them, so it is noted here rather than balanced away.
- **Spare Components.** Dust wears everything down steadily, so a growing base uses spare Components fast: the reference Campaign keeps 30 in stock, topped up by every Launch, until a Workshop takes that over. With fewer, Maintenance waits for spares, solar arrays wear down to Broken, and a Night can then empty the battery banks. With one Workshop the long-game play guide's stock fell to 3 on about Earth Day 2,685 and recovered, for a cause never found; with two and a concentrator tower it no longer does, and its low after Earth Day 2,500 is 25.
- **One extraction plant.** It can make oxygen or silicon, but not both at once, so wafer and circuit board production slows while the oxygen is topped up.
- **Water.** The water wall takes 300 kg; after filling it the reference Campaign's water fell to 31 kg (under nine Earth Days) before the next Expedition's ice came through. A player should send the Rover again as soon as the wall is filled.
- **The first Launch's Funding.** With the Programme Grant above what any one Launch can cost, nothing stops a player spending most of it on the first Launch but the hint; one who does can't afford the export Plants, and has no way to earn more.

## The catalogue's consistency rules

`test/catalogue-rules.test.ts` holds the data catalogue to these rules with the other tests, so that a bad addition fails with a clear reason, naming the item and the rule, and nobody has to replay the game to find it:

- **`design-has-a-purpose`:** every Design brings something the long-game play guide uses (`designsTheLongGameUses`), or something from another Design does the same job, so that it offers a choice.
- **`design-not-beaten`:** no Design is beaten outright by something at least as cheap, in Science on the way to it, money and mass, and at least as good at the same job, as the pacing report works it out.
- **`export-can-be-sold`:** every Export can be made by some Plant, and sold within the Return Mass.
- **`bulk-resource-has-a-use`:** every bulk resource goes into some Plant, or is an Export.

An item may break a rule only if it is on the exemption list in `test/catalogue-rules.ts`, with the reason. An exemption for something that no longer breaks the rule fails too, so the list can't go stale.
