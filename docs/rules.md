# How the game works

This is how a Campaign plays. It describes the rules, not the numbers: rates, masses, prices, thresholds, durations and Science costs are set in the data catalogue (`src/catalogue/`, one file for each domain), and the game shows them where they matter. [`GLOSSARY.md`](../GLOSSARY.md) defines the terms in capitals.

## Conventions

These hold throughout, so the sections below don't repeat them:

- **Refusals give the reason.** Any command that can't be carried out (an item that won't fit on the Launch manifest, a Structure placed where it can't stand, a Design that isn't unlocked) is refused, and the game says why.
- **Notifications never pause the game.** They appear in the list at the bottom right. "Skip to next event" jumps straight to the next notable moment: Night falling, a Construction commissioned, a warning, a Milestone (with a new Act, if it brings one), each leg of the Lander's round trip, a Solar Particle Event's warning, start and end, an Expedition leaving or returning, a Research Task, a round of the observing programme or a Greenhouse's round of growing completed, a Greenhouse's round lost, or a Structure needing Maintenance.
- **Screens don't pause the game either**: the Launch manifest, Research Tasks, Tech Tree and Expeditions screens all let it run on.
- **Running out is forecast and warned of.** For oxygen, water, Habitat power, the Radiation Dose and spare Components, the status panel shows when the limit will be reached, and warnings come at three levels of time left (the data catalogue sets them).
- **Chance is seeded.** Random events are the same for the same seed and choices. Add `?seed=<number>` to the address to replay a Campaign exactly.

## Screen and controls

- **Top bar:** the game clock, game speed, "Skip to next event", the Funding and Science balances, the Launch manifest, Research Tasks and Tech Tree buttons, the Guide, and Save and Load.
- **Status panel (left):** the current Act and Milestones, the Mission Director, the base's power, stock and bulk resources, the Lander and Expeditions, and the running Research Task, those queued behind it in order and, once the base has a telescope, the observing programme, above the build menu. Its lines are kept short: the Living line says where the Mission Director lives and whether the Habitat has power, the Components in stock are listed two to a row, and a figure that needs its reason, such as a stock that is rising but won't last the Night, keeps the reason for hovering over the line.
- **Base panel (right):** the Robots, Machines and Rovers, and below them the Structures, with their Condition, between the Work panel and the notification list. Structures are listed together by type, under a heading with how many there are, and each type folds away on its own, as the sections do; a dot by a type's heading means one of them is Broken or low on Condition.
- Each section of the status and Base panels folds away when you click its title and stays folded on your next visit; a folded status section keeps its headline figures beside its title, such as the power to spare, how long the oxygen and water last, and which bulk resource will run out soonest, and a dot by a folded title means something in it needs attention. Hover over a self-sufficiency figure or the time left in the Lunation for detail.
- **Lines hold still, even at 20x.** Rows keep their order, and a line keeps the tallest height it has taken while shown, so that when its text gets shorter, however quickly or slowly, the lines below it never move back up; it gives the room back when its section is folded, a Campaign is loaded or the window changes size. Work that is done (a Construction commissioned, a Structure dismantled, Maintenance finished, a Machine or the Rover parked and plugged in, the Lander unloaded or loaded) stays in the Work panel, marked "Done", for a few real seconds before its row goes, while work that stops for any other reason, such as a cancelled Construction, goes at once; the Work panel stays in place saying "No work under way" while there is none, and the Solar Particle Event line keeps its place in the Mission Director section, saying "none announced" between events. A line with nothing to say is left out rather than kept to say so: the next Milestone's line goes for good once none is left, and the Expedition's line, the Research queue's and the Science earned a day aren't there until the first Expedition is under way, the first Research Task queued or Science first left unspent, and the first Science being earned. Once one has shown it stays, saying "none under way", "none" or "none being earned" between times, as a bulk resource's line does, so that the lines below it move only that once. The current Milestone's hint shows its first two lines, with a "Show all" button under it for the rest that the keyboard reaches too; it stays as the player leaves it, folded or whole, as the Milestones go by, so that it changes height only when they ask. A bulk resource's line, once it has shown, stays until a Campaign is loaded, even with none left.
- **Guide:** explains, without leaving the game, how the base works (power and Night, repairs, keeping the Mission Director alive, Launches and Funding, Science), what every Structure does, with its figures, Components and the Design it needs, what every Component is for, what the Robot, Machines and Rover do, and what each technology unlocks. Every figure and list in it comes from the data catalogue.
- **What things are for, where you choose them:** hovering over a Structure in the build menu says what it does and what it is built from; each item on the Launch manifest says what it is for (the Structures built from a Component and how many each takes, what spare Components do, how long oxygen or water lasts the Mission Director); each Tech Tree card says what unlocking it brings, with what each of its Structures does; and the Research Tasks screen lists what Science buys next, and on each task the Designs its Science would bring within reach or, if it pays too little for any, the cheapest it builds towards and how far short it leaves you. These say the same as the Guide.
- **Structure pop-up:** click a Structure on the map, commissioned, under Construction or being dismantled, to open a pop-up beside it saying what it is, what it does and what state it is in: its stage of Construction or dismantling and how far it has got, its Condition and any Maintenance, what it is rated to generate, store and draw and what its power network makes and uses now, a battery bank's charge, what a storage tank holds, a Plant's recipe and what it makes, a Habitat's residents, shielding and Radiation Dose rate, a light's state, whether a telescope is observing and how the observing programme is going, how far a Greenhouse's round has got, how many of a parking area's bays are taken, and a solar array's Dust coating. It updates live, and once the Structure is commissioned it offers the same choices as its entry in the Base panel (recipe, switch off or on, Move in or Move here, Dismantle). Clicking another Structure switches to it; clicking empty ground, pressing Esc or its close button closes it, and so does the Structure going once dismantled. A drag moves the camera rather than counting as a click, and while a Structure is being placed, a click places it instead. Regolith shielding and a water wall cover the Habitat, so clicking any of them opens the Habitat's pop-up, with a button to switch to each of them, and one back from theirs.
- **Camera:** drag with the left mouse button to pan, drag with the right button to rotate, and use the wheel to zoom from the whole map down to a single Robot.

## Milestones

Milestones guide a new player through the Campaign. They carry no rewards and never end it, and there is no victory condition: the last is celebrated, and the Campaign goes on. The status panel shows the current Milestone (the earliest not yet reached) with a hint saying what to do next, and the one after it. Beside the Act's name it says which of the Act's Milestones the current one is, such as "Milestone 3 of 5", counted in the catalogue's order whatever has been reached. Where the current Milestone has something to measure, a line with a bar under its name shows how far along it is: for self-sufficiency in oxygen, water or spare Components, the share of what was used that the base made; for Return Mass tripled, the Return Mass and the Exports aboard or to load, each against the 300 kg to carry; and for the Large-scale Crew Habitat, the oxygen and water in stock against what it is stocked with and the Mission Director's reserve, with the room the base has for each while that is too little. The other Milestones are simply done or not, and have none. These lines are guidance: only its condition reaches a Milestone. A Milestone is reached the first time its condition holds, in any order, and stays reached even if the condition later stops holding. The data catalogue lists them, with their hints:

1. **Power storage before the first Night:** a Structure that stores power is commissioned (even if the first Night has already fallen).
2. **Habitat commissioned, Mission Director moved in.**
3. **Lander departs:** the Lander takes off for Earth for the first time.
4. **Habitat shielded:** Regolith shielding is commissioned over the Habitat the Mission Director lives in, or they live in a Buried Habitat, which needs none.
5. **Landing pad:** a landing pad is commissioned.
6. **First Research Task:** a Research Task is completed.
7. **First Export:** an Export is sold as the Lander reaches Earth.
8. **Oxygen self-sufficiency:** as the status panel judges it (see [Self-sufficiency](#self-sufficiency)).
9. **Water self-sufficiency:** the same, for water.
10. **Workshop running:** a Plant that makes spare Components has started a batch.
11. **Self-sufficiency in spare Components:** as the status panel judges it (see [Self-sufficiency](#self-sufficiency)).
12. **Metals sold:** an Export of metals is sold as the Lander reaches Earth.

### Acts

Each Milestone belongs to an Act, set in the data catalogue. The first nine above make up Act 1, and the last three Act 2: Independence; `docs/balancing.md` has the Acts planned after them. Like Milestones, Acts carry no rewards and never end the Campaign.

### The last Milestone

The last Milestone, Act 4's **Large-scale Crew Habitat commissioned**, is what the Campaign builds towards. Its Design, Large-scale Crew Habitats, needs a Buried Habitat and Growing halls, and costs little Science; what it takes is Funding and the base's oxygen and water. Its 24 vault sections, four deep life support cores and two airlocks come by Launch, about nine Launches' worth. As its Assembly starts it is stocked all at once with 600 kg of oxygen and 2,500 kg of water from the base's stocks, by a water wall's rule: it waits, and the Work panel says how much more of which it needs, until taking both leaves the Mission Director a fortnight's reserve of each, and says to build another tank if the base can't hold that much. Nobody moves in, as Crew are still to come: it draws no power and has no "Move here" button.

Once it is commissioned and every other Milestone is reached, as the last of them is reached, a celebration opens over everything, saying the base has moved on to its next phase, and the game pauses until the player presses Carry on, which sets it going again at the speed it had. So a habitat built before the Mission Director has moved underground is celebrated only once they have. It comes only as a Milestone is reached, so loading a save that has reached them all shows none. The Campaign then carries on as it does once every Milestone is reached.

The current Act is the Act of the current Milestone, or the last Act once every Milestone is reached. It isn't saved: it comes from the Milestones reached, so a save, however old, loads in the Act they imply: one with Act 1's Milestones all reached loads straight into Act 2, and says nothing of it beyond the panel. The Milestones section of the status panel shows its name and what it asks, above the current Milestone. The line for the next Milestone also names that Milestone's Act if it is a later one. Once every Milestone is reached, the section says so, and that the Campaign carries on, with one Act or several.

A notification marks the start of a new Act, the moment reaching a Milestone moves the current Act on. Reaching a Milestone of a later Act early doesn't do so while one of an earlier Act is left, and passing over an Act whose Milestones were all reached early gives only the one notification, for the Act that is now current. No notification comes for Act 1 at the landing, or on loading a save.

## Construction

To plan a Construction, pick a Structure in the build menu, move its translucent preview over the map and click to place it; right-click or Esc cancels. The Robot carries it out by itself through the stages of Construction, and the Work panel shows each one:

- **Site Preparation:** the Robot drives the excavator to the site and activates it, and the excavator grades the ground by itself while the Robot gets on with other work. For a Structure the regolith printer prints, such as Regolith shielding or a sintered landing pad, it also digs the Regolith the printer will use, 6 hours more, so printed Structures cost excavator time and wear rather than Components or the base's Regolith stock. This is part of Site Preparation, not the digging that fills the stock (below).
- **Component delivery and Assembly:** the Robot loads the Components at the stockpile beside the Lander, carries them over and assembles them.
- **Commissioning**, which can fail.
- **Cable:** every Construction after the first that uses power also has the Robot lay power cable from the nearest powered Structure (one on a network with a solar array or battery bank). It pays the cable out along the way it walks, so the cable goes round anything in the way. Only Structures joined by cable share power. If a Structure that uses power is ever left without a source, the Robot lays it a new cable as soon as there is one.

A Construction whose Components aren't in the base's stock waits, with the reason in the Work panel, and the Robot moves on to the next. "Cancel" in the Work panel removes an unfinished Construction at once and returns its unassembled Components. "Dismantle" beside a commissioned Structure in the Base panel has the Robot take it apart and carry spare Components back to the stockpile, rather than what it was built from: one from most Structures, and more from a large or complex one, up to six from a Crew habitat, while one made from Regolith alone gives nothing back. Its description in the build menu and the Guide, and the Dismantle button's tooltip, say how many. The Structure keeps working until the Robot starts. Its cables go with it, but anything that drew power through it stays on the network: its cables are joined up as it goes, so dismantling a battery bank or a solar array never cuts the Habitat off from the rest of the base's power.

Robots, and the Machines and Rover they drive, take the shortest way round the Lander and every Structure's footprint, keeping half a metre clear of it. That includes Structures still under Construction, from the moment they are planned, so that nobody crosses ground being graded or built on, and Structures being dismantled, until they are taken apart. They only step onto a footprint where their work is, such as beside the Lander on a landing pad, and make their way out of one, and round it, if they ever find themselves inside it. Squeezing between two footprints takes a gap of more than a metre for a Robot, and about 4 to 5 m for a Machine or the Rover. If Structures close a spot in completely, with no gap wide enough, whoever is going there goes straight through them rather than never arriving: leave gaps when you pack Structures round a courtyard.

Nothing can be built over the stockpile, the Lander, the landing site or the Rover, and while you place a Structure, the build menu shows the Plume Ejecta danger zone round the Lander (placing in it is allowed). A few Structures can't be dismantled while something depends on them: the Habitat the Mission Director lives in, a shielded Habitat until its shielding is gone, Regolith shielding until any water wall over it is gone, a landing pad while the Lander stands on it, and a radio telescope array while any antenna field joins it.

## Power

Solar arrays generate only in Daylight. Battery banks store any surplus and cover any shortfall. The status panel adds up the base's generation, use and stored energy across its networks, leading with the power to spare, or the shortfall, as at Night (for example "+101.1 kW" before "137.6 made, 36.5 used"), and says when the first network's stored power will run out. That forecast follows the Daylight and Night ahead, so stored power that doesn't run out says "lasts the Night", in Daylight too. The Habitat can draw only on its own network, so while the Mission Director lives in a Habitat and another network has a solar array or battery bank, a Habitat line below says what the Habitat's network has stored and when it will run out, and the folded Power section adds how full its battery banks are, for example "Habitat 56%".

When a network can't cover everything, power goes in this order:

1. The Habitat. It works only with all the power it draws; part of it is the same as none.
2. Other Structures, which lose power in the order they were commissioned. A storage tank is one of these, at Night only (see [Plants](#plants), under storage limits), a Greenhouse or an automated experiments lab, in Daylight only, and a radio telescope array, by day and at Night (see [Research and the Tech Tree](#research-and-the-tech-tree)).
3. Heaters of Machines and Rovers plugged in to the network, at Night.
4. Lights, telescopes, Greenhouses' lamps, automated experiments labs and infrared telescopes, at Night, in the order they were commissioned (see below, and [Research and the Tech Tree](#research-and-the-tech-tree)). Like lights, telescopes and Greenhouses keep the battery banks' reserve.
5. Plants other than a Greenhouse, an automated experiments lab or an infrared telescope, which run only on power that nothing else needs: in Daylight while the battery banks would still be full 24 hours before Night falls, if what is generated and drawn stays as it is, so that with surplus enough they run as the banks charge, and otherwise wait until the banks are far enough ahead (within those 24 hours, only once the banks are full); and at Night while the battery banks hold more than everything else needs through the rest of the Night and the same reserve the lights keep, counting the heater of every Machine and Rover that isn't yet plugged in, as it may be parked before morning. A heater not plugged in draws nothing, so as the Night goes on that frees a little: once the Plants have used all there is to spare, the one that draws least runs for a game-minute each time that much has built up, and the Base panel says it is running on and off, with little stored power to spare. Any other stays idle. Battery banks beyond what the Night needs keep the Plants running into it.

Light Masts switch on by themselves at Night, light the ground round them, and draw power only while on. They only help you see: work carries on in the dark without them. At Night a light stays on only while its network's battery banks hold more than everything else on the network needs through the rest of the Night and a reserve after it; otherwise it switches itself off to save power, the last-commissioned first, so lights never leave the Habitat, or anything else, without power. The Base panel shows a light off to save power, the power figures and forecasts leave it out, and a notification says when the lights on a network start switching off.

One battery bank can't carry the Habitat through a Night; the starting stock holds enough for one Night once they are charged. The Lander can't bring more in time if it leaves in the first Daylight, so build them all before moving in, or wait aboard until the next Daylight.

## The Mission Director

The Mission Director starts aboard the Lander, with an inflatable Habitat kit in the starting stock. Once the Habitat is commissioned and powered, "Move in" beside it moves them in at once, and they can't go back aboard. The Lander is then free to fly.

Later they can move again: "Move here" beside any other Habitat, in the Base panel and its pop-up, moves them there at once. It has to be commissioned, powered, not Broken and not to be dismantled, or the move is refused and the game says why. Nothing stops a move into an unshielded Habitat: the dose rate there is what it is, the pop-up warns that moving there would raise it and how soon the limit would then come, and once they are there the forecast and warnings follow. The Radiation Dose they have taken goes with them. Only the Habitat they live in now can kill them by losing power, and it can't be dismantled until they leave it. The Habitat they left can then be dismantled, its water wall and shielding first; until then it draws its power as before, so a base keeping both has to carry both through the Night.

- **Oxygen and water.** The base starts with enough for about a month. Aboard the Lander they are safe indefinitely; once the Mission Director lives in the Habitat they use some every Earth Day. The base holds only so much of each (see [Storage limits](#storage-limits)).
- **Habitat power.** Once they live in it, the Habitat must stay powered. An empty, unpowered Habitat harms nobody.
- **Radiation Dose.** None aboard the Lander, and a steady rate in the Habitat, cut by shielding (see [Radiation](#radiation)).

If oxygen or water runs out, the Habitat they live in loses power, or the Radiation Dose reaches the limit, the Mission Director dies at once and the Campaign is over. The game-over screen says how they died, and "Start a new Campaign" starts again from the landing (with the same seed, if the address gives one).

## Radiation

- **Regolith shielding:** pick it in the build menu and place it on a commissioned Habitat. The excavator grades the ground round it and digs the Regolith for it there, then the Robot drives the regolith printer over and activates it, and the printer builds the shielding up layer by layer: no Components and no cable. It cuts the dose rate sharply.
- **Water wall** (the Water-wall shielding Design): its bladders come by Launch and go over a Habitat's Regolith shielding (either kind). They are filled from the base's water stock as Assembly starts, which must leave the Mission Director a reserve of drinking water; until there is enough, the Construction waits, and the Work panel says how much more water it needs. The water stays in the wall even if the Construction is cancelled or the wall dismantled. It cuts the dose rate much further again.
- **Buried Habitat** (the Buried Habitat Design, past Water-wall shielding): a Habitat of its own, sunk in a trench and heaped over with Regolith, and the only way to live out a long Campaign. Its vault sections and deep life support core come by Launch, too heavy for one, and cost more than anything else on the Launch manifest, so it takes planning for. The excavator takes Earth Days to dig it in. It takes no Regolith shielding or water wall: placing either over it is refused, as it needs neither. Move the Mission Director in with "Move here"; the dose rate falls to a tenth of a water wall's, but the Radiation Dose already taken never falls, so the sooner the first Habitat was shielded, the longer the Campaign can run.
- **Vault cistern** (the Vault cistern Design, past Growing halls, a Buried Habitat and Bulk supply tanks): cells of water that go round a commissioned Buried Habitat and nothing else, centred on it, as a water wall does over its shielding. The excavator grades the ground, its cells come by Launch, and they are filled from the base's water stock as Assembly starts, all at once, which must leave the Mission Director the same reserve as a water wall. So the base has to hold it all first: until it does, the Construction waits, and the Work panel says how much more water it needs and, if more than the base can hold, to build another water tank. It takes the dose rate down to a fifth of a Buried Habitat's. A Broken one stops doing so, and it has to be dismantled before the Buried Habitat is. The water stays in it, like a water wall's. Science from Research Tasks doesn't reach its Design: it takes a Greenhouse or a growing hall.
- **Solar Particle Events** come at random, with a few hours' warning, and multiply the dose rate while they last. Aboard the Lander it stays zero and shielding keeps it low, but in an unshielded Habitat one can take half the dose limit. They multiply a Buried Habitat's rate like any other. The forecasts and warnings account for them.

Shielding, the water wall and the vault cistern are the only Structures that may overlap another, and only the Habitat they cover (and, for the water wall, its shielding). The Base panel shows a Habitat as buried, shielded or water-walled, and a Buried Habitat with a vault cistern; a Broken water wall or shielding stops protecting it, and a Broken Buried Habitat protects nobody either, though like any Broken Habitat it has already killed anyone living in it.

## Launches and the Lander

The Mission Director starts with the Programme Grant as Funding. "Launch manifest" opens the screen where you choose what the next Launch brings: Components whose Design is unlocked, oxygen and water, and, once the Robots Design is unlocked, extra Robots. Bars show the manifest's Payload Mass against the Launch's limit and its cost (the Launch fee plus each item's price, or nothing while empty) against the Funding, and stay in sight as the list scrolls. Each item's row says how much the base already has of what it brings, kept up to date as the game runs on, with "None" fainter so that what it does have stands out, and apart from that anything on a Lander that has left Earth or waiting aboard to be unloaded. This only informs: nothing stops the player ordering more.

The items are listed in groups: first Supplies, the oxygen, water and spare Components a base keeps on buying, then the Tech Tree's categories in its order, each with the other items of its Designs, so that a Robot is under Mobility, where the Robots Design is. A group shows once it has an item unlocked, always in the same place, and items keep their order within it, so an unlocked Design adds rows but never reorders them. Once more than 30 items are listed, which is more than a Campaign starts with, a box beside the bars finds an item: type part of its name, of what it is for, or of its group's name, and only the items with every word typed stay listed, with how many that is out of those listed. Only the player changes what is typed: the game running on, even at 20x, leaves it and the caret alone, and it stays while the screen is closed or the Exports tab is open, until "Clear" empties it.

Once the Mission Director has moved into a Habitat, "Send the Lander" sends it on its round trip: it flies to Earth, turns around there and flies back to land on the same spot. You can change the manifest until the Lander leaves Earth; then it locks and its cost comes off the Funding. It can never cost more than the Lander can pay as it leaves (see [Exports](#exports)). When the Lander lands, its Components join the base's stock and its oxygen and water the life support stocks, up to their [storage limits](#storage-limits), and a new, empty manifest starts. The base carries on while it is away. Running out of Funding never ends the Campaign by itself.

**Plume Ejecta.** Every take-off and landing at the landing site blasts the ground round it, wearing down the Condition of every Structure within reach.

**Landing pads** stop that. A landing pad can go anywhere clear of other Structures, but its pad panels and beacon must come by Launch. Once it is commissioned, the Lander lands and takes off there with no Plume Ejecta, and the danger zone disappears. On the pad, oxygen and water still arrive as the Lander lands, but the Components stay aboard until an unloading Work Order has a Robot carry them to the stockpile, and the Lander can't leave until it has. If the pad is dismantled or Broken, the Lander uses the landing site again.

## Exports

Wafers, circuit boards, metals, propellant and precision optics are Exports. A Plant's rate is what limits circuit boards, but metals are cheap to make and sell for little by the kilogram, so they run into the Return Mass instead: they are for filling what the circuit boards leave of it, and a full Return Mass of them doesn't pay the Launch fee. The Launch manifest screen's Exports tab sets how many kilograms of each to load, against the Lander's Return Mass and the base's stock, with what they will sell for. Its Aboard column shows the kilograms of each already on the Lander, until they are sold. They can't be changed while the Lander is away.

Propellant raises the Return Mass. The Lander carries 100 kg on what it brings from Earth, and a kilogram more for every 2 kg of propellant in stock, up to 400 kg. It burns only what it needs, as it takes off. Propellant loaded to be sold doesn't count, as it can't be burned as well. So the Return Mass reaches its 400 kg with 600 kg of propellant to burn, and one trip can use at most 1,000 kg, burning 600 kg to lift 400 kg of propellant to sell. 2 kg of propellant would sell for $2.40M, so while it is short it is worth burning only to carry circuit boards or precision optics, not metals. A base with more than it can sell can burn it for anything. How much the base can hold has a limit (see [Storage limits](#plants)).

- **At the landing site**, Exports stay in the base's stock until take-off, then go aboard (as much as is left).
- **On a landing pad**, setting them raises a loading Work Order, and a Robot carries them aboard. Until it picks them up, changing the amounts changes what it carries and clearing them cancels the loading; once it has them, changes and sending the Lander are refused until they are aboard. You can then set more for another round of loading.

The Exports are sold when the Lander reaches Earth, before the manifest is paid for as it leaves, so the manifest's cost may be up to the Funding balance plus what the Exports aboard and to load will sell for, and the Launch manifest screen counts their value alongside the Funding. The Exports to load count for as much of each as the base still has in stock, as the Lander takes no more. They can still be lowered, and the stock can run short of them as Plants use it, so the Lander can't be sent while the manifest costs more than they and the Funding come to; it says so, and the Imports tab warns of it. Once it is sent, they go aboard before anything else can use them.

## Condition and Maintenance

Every commissioned Structure, Machine, Rover and Robot has a Condition, from 100% down. The Base panel shows it, rounded to the whole percent. Things that wear it down:

- **Dust:** a rate for each hour something operates, set by type. A Structure to do with power operates only while powered; one without, such as a landing pad, always does. Machines and Rovers wear while working or driven, Robots while not idle.
- **Plume Ejecta** (see above).
- **Micrometeorites:** strikes at random, without warning, each hitting one Structure, Machine, Rover or Robot and taking a random share of its Condition.
- **The cold of Night:** Machines and Rovers left unpowered (see [Machines and the Rover](#machines-and-the-rover)).
- **Dust coating:** solar arrays also gather Dust coating in Daylight, which cuts their output on top of any wear.

Below a threshold, things work less well in step with their Condition: solar arrays generate less, battery banks store less (losing any energy above what they can now hold), Light Masts light a smaller circle, Plants run slower, telescopes observe more slowly, and Machines and Robots work and move more slowly. The power figures and forecasts follow. The Habitat, shielding, landing pads and storage tanks work fully until Broken.

At 0% a Structure or Machine is **Broken**: it stays where it is but stops working. A Broken battery bank loses its stored energy, a Broken storage tank loses its share of the stock it holds, and a Broken Habitat loses its power and shielding, which kills the Mission Director if they live in it. A Broken Machine gives up the Construction stage it was on until another is free or it is repaired. A Broken Rover can't be driven. Robots are never Broken.

**Maintenance.** Once something's Condition falls below the Maintenance threshold, or it is Broken, a Maintenance Work Order is raised for it. The Robot loads spare Components at the stockpile, carries them over and restores it to 100%, using spare Components in proportion to the Condition restored. If the base's stock has too few, the Work Order waits, the Work panel says how many more it needs and to put spare Components on the Launch manifest or make them in a Workshop (see [Plants](#plants)), and the Robot does other work meanwhile. The player never repairs anything: a line at the top of the Base panel's Structures and Robots & Machines sections says repairs are automatic, hovering over a Condition says the same, and the notifications that something needs Maintenance or is Broken say the Robot will repair it. A solar array whose Dust coating passes a threshold is cleaned the same way, without spares, on the same visit as any Maintenance it is due. Nothing that is to be dismantled is maintained, and a Machine busy with a Construction stage is maintained once it is idle again, unless it is Broken.

**Spare Components forecast.** The status panel shows how long the spares will last at the current rate of wear (including the average Micrometeorite damage), after the spares already claimed. While a Workshop is making more than is used it says how fast they are rising instead. It reads what the Workshop makes the way the oxygen and water forecasts read their Plants: a batch late, as a batch's spare comes only as it finishes, and counting on none through a Night. So in Daylight it follows the rate to nightfall, then the wear through the Night, and so on through the Lunations ahead; at Night it looks as far as the next Daylight, and says "enough until Daylight" if they last. A Workshop switched off, Broken or waiting for metals counts as making nothing, and the line names one that is switched off. Each warning is given once, until more spares arrive, by Launch or from a Workshop, or the forecast recovers well clear of the warning levels.

## Robots

Robots carry out Work Orders by themselves, in this order:

1. Repairing themselves.
2. Maintenance, in the order it was raised.
3. Parking Machines and Rovers before and during Night.
4. Everything else (Constructions, dismantling, unloading and loading the Lander) in the order you planned it.
5. Taking Machines and Rovers with nothing else to do to a parking area's bays, in the rest of Daylight.
6. Digging Regolith, only when there is nothing else to do.

Except to repair itself, a Robot finishes the Work Order it is on before taking a higher-priority one. Below a low Condition, a Robot stops, collects spare Components and repairs itself where the Mission Director lives (the Habitat, or the Lander while they are aboard), then takes up its Work Order again. Short of spares it makes a makeshift repair and carries on slowly.

**Extra Robots.** Unlocking the Robots Design offers a Robot on the Launch manifest. Each one shipped climbs down the Lander's ladder as it lands and walks to the stockpile, after which it works like the first. The Lander can't leave while a Robot is still climbing down. Robots share the Work Orders: each claims the next one that no other Robot holds, so several Constructions can go ahead at once. The Base panel and notifications number them ("Robot 1", "Robot 2").

## Machines and the Rover

The excavator and the regolith printer are Machines: a Robot drives them to a site and activates them, and they work by themselves. The Rover arrives in the starting cargo and is used for Expeditions.

A Machine or Rover standing close to a powered, unbroken Structure is **plugged in**: at Night its heater draws from that network, and the Base panel shows it as powered. Once parking has started, an idle Machine that is plugged in and that the Robots aren't still to take to a bay shows as "parked for Night": it stays there until Daylight unless a stage of Construction or Maintenance needs it. One that isn't plugged in, or whose network can't supply its heater, wears down in the cold. Some hours before Night falls, the Robots start parking each idle Machine that isn't plugged in, then the Rover, beside the nearest powered Structure, and during Night they do the same whenever one becomes idle away from power. The heaters draw on the same battery banks as the Habitat.

**Parking areas.** A parking area is graded, marked ground with four bays, each with a power socket. It is there from the start, needs no Science, and its four bay sockets come by Launch (60 kg, $4M in all); it goes up in about 4 hours of work. What stands in a bay of one that is commissioned, isn't Broken and is cabled to a network with a solar array or battery bank is plugged in. Once its network can also keep heaters on through the Night ahead (its battery banks, with what they hold and what its solar arrays still make before Night falls, would carry everything on it and the heaters of every Machine and Rover through to the end of the Night), parking sends each Machine or Rover that needs it to the free bay nearest it, in preference to the nearest powered Structure, as long as the bay is no more than 25 m further off than that Structure (the data catalogue's detour), so that the drive and the walk back still fit before Night falls. On a network with no battery bank, or whose battery banks won't last the Night, it takes nothing, as parking there would be no better than beside the nearest powered Structure. A bay is free while nothing stands in it and no other park Work Order is taking anything to it, so no two are ever sent to the same bay. With no such parking area, or every bay taken, parking goes beside the nearest powered Structure as before.

The parking area is where idle Machines and Rovers live, so that they aren't left about the base. In Daylight, before the hours of parking for the Night, the Robots take each Machine or Rover that stands in no bay to the free bay nearest it, plugged in elsewhere or not, as soon as it has nothing else to do, however far off the bay is. A Machine has more to do while it is digging, while it is the excavator and the base needs digging (so it digs the stock full before it goes back, once a dig, rather than back and forth), which in the hours before Night includes the stock wanting a top-up (below), or while a Construction's current stage is one its type of Machine carries out and waits for nothing but a Machine; one waiting for Components or for water may wait for weeks, so it doesn't keep a Machine out. The Rover has more to do while an Expedition is planned for it or it is away on one. These drives come after the work you planned and before digging, so tidying never holds up the base. In the hours before Night falls, the Robots also park in a bay each idle one that is plugged in beside another Structure, as long as the bay is within the detour. Once in a bay, a Machine or Rover stays there until it is given work. Nothing is moved at Night that is plugged in, when the drive would leave it in the cold; it goes to a bay in the next Daylight. Every drive takes a Robot's time and wears the Machine by Dust, so put the parking area near where the Machines work.

## Bulk resources, digging and Expeditions

The base keeps bulk resources at the stockpile, measured in kilograms: Regolith, water ice, metals, silicon, wafers and circuit boards. The status panel's Bulk resources section shows them, a line each, with how fast any that is changing rises or falls a day, as the Plants and digging are running now, and roughly how long any that is falling will last. A bulk resource has no line until the base first has some, or is making or using some, and then keeps it, dimmed once there is less than a kilogram, so that the list is only as long as it needs to be. One that is falling and will run out within a few Earth Days shows in the warning colour, with the dot by the section's title; one with none left doesn't, as nothing may want it. Folded, the section says how long the one that will run out soonest lasts and how much there is of the first other in stock, such as "Silicon 2d · Regolith 1250 kg".

**Digging.** Whenever Regolith falls below 1000 kg and the excavator is free, a Robot with nothing else to do drives it to clear ground beside the stockpile and sets it digging. It digs on until the stock is full at 2000 kg, so the Plants can draw it down for days before the Robot is needed again. It tips the Regolith onto a heap beside the stockpile's mat, on the side away from the landing site, which grows with the stock to about 1.6 m in radius (3.2 m across) and 1.1 m high, drawn larger than the mat, and shrinks as the Plants use it; with none in stock there is no heap. The excavator digs just beyond it, and Robots stand clear of it, both to load and unload beside the stockpile and to maintain or park a Machine beside it. The heap is outside the stockpile's footprint, so a Structure can be planned and built over it, which hides it. Digging always gives way to Site Preparation, Maintenance and parking, and carries on afterwards only if the stock has fallen below 1000 kg.

**Regolith's limit.** The heap holds 2000 kg, which is Regolith's storage limit, and the status panel's Bulk resources show it against that (for example "Regolith 1250.0 of 2000 kg"). A Regolith bin (the Continuous excavation Design, past Large propellant production) adds 3000 kg once it is commissioned. It is a storage tank (see [Plants](#plants)) that needs no power and takes no cable, and loses nothing at Night. Each level above rises by what the bins add: with one bin, digging starts below 4000 kg, carries on to 5000 kg, and tops up before Night below 4950 kg. The heap is drawn no larger than it is with 2000 kg; each bin shows its share of the stock filling it.

**Bucket-wheel digger.** A Plant from the same Design that digs 30 kg of Regolith an hour by itself, with no Robot or Machine, for 4 kW while it runs. As a Plant it runs only on power to spare, at Night too, so with battery banks to spare it keeps a base's Plants supplied while the excavator is parked. Its Regolith goes into the same stock: once that is at its limit it waits, and the Base panel says "waiting for room to store it: Regolith is 2000.0 of 2000 kg".

**Digging before Night.** The excavator is parked through the Night, and digs nothing until Daylight, as digging beside the stockpile would leave it in the cold; Plants with stored power to spare go on drawing the stock down all the while. So in the 30 hours before the Robots start parking for the Night, digging also starts once the stock is below 1950 kg, an hour's digging short of full, and the excavator stays out of its bay only while the stock is below that or it is digging, so a stock already near full doesn't keep it out for those hours. The stock goes into the Night full, or as near as a worn excavator and the Robots' other work allow. A full stock lasts a Night of 9.5 Earth Days only while the Plants running through it use less than about 210 kg of Regolith an Earth Day (a large propellant plant alone uses 840), so a base that uses more still runs out before morning, unless a bucket-wheel digger (below) digs for it; its Plants then wait for Daylight, when the excavator digs again. While the base needs digging and the excavator can't, the status panel's Regolith line says why: "not dug while the excavator is parked for Night", "is busy with Construction" (such as a Buried Habitat's 120 hours of Site Preparation), "is Broken" or "is undergoing Maintenance".

**Ice store.** The base's water ice shows in insulated containers beside the stockpile's mat, a quarter of the way round from the heap. A container fills for each 200 kg, from one with the first ice up to seven, and they empty again as the Plants use it; with none in stock there are no containers. More than seven containers' worth still counts in full, since the stock has no limit. Robots stand clear of them, and the excavator digs beyond them. They are outside the stockpile's footprint, so a Structure can be planned and built over them, which hides them.

**Expeditions.** The Expeditions screen offers each type the base can send, with how long it takes and what it brings back, and starting one sends a Robot off the map in the Rover. It finishes the Work Order it holds, drives off the nearest map edge, is away for those Earth Days, then drives back and unloads what it brought beside the stockpile. Water ice is offered from the start: 200 kg in 3 Earth Days. Prospecting Expeditions, a cheap Design past Robots, offers 30 kg of metals or 20 kg of silicon in 6 Earth Days. It can be had before the first extraction plant comes, so it can bring a base its first metals and silicon, though until a second Robot lands, sending one leaves the base without a Robot for those days; it brings less than one extraction plant makes in that time, so once the Plants run it tops the base up without taking their place. Survey Expeditions offers a Survey, which earns 25 Science in 10 Earth Days and brings nothing back to store. None carries any risk. Only one Expedition can be under way at a time, and not while the Rover is Broken. While away, the Robot takes no Work Orders and doesn't repair itself, and the cold and Micrometeorites pass both of them by. The Rover wears down by Dust as it drives, but never so far that it can't come back.

## Plants

A Plant is a Structure that runs a recipe by itself once commissioned: it turns bulk resources (or Regolith) into others, or into oxygen or water, or, in a Workshop or an array works, into Components, or, in a cold-trap ice line, takes nothing in and brings water ice from the ground. Its Components come by Launch, and a Robot lays it a cable like any other. It draws power only while running, last of everything on its network, so it can never take power from the Habitat.

A Plant works in batches, each a game-hour of its recipe, or more if the recipe says so: a Workshop's makes one whole spare Component, and an array works' one whole solar panel or array mast, and each takes a whole Earth Day. It starts one only once a batch's worth of every input is in stock, and takes them as it starts; the batch's outputs come as it finishes, a batch's length later at full Condition and longer below it, and the next batch starts straight after if the inputs are there. A batch needs power to spare the whole time it runs: without it, as at Night once the battery banks have none to spare, the batch waits with the work done on it kept, and finishes once there is power again. A Broken Plant's batch waits for its repair. A batch is lost if its Plant is dismantled.

The Base panel says what each Plant is doing: running, with the time left of its batch and what it makes and uses an hour, or for a Workshop what each batch makes and takes; waiting for an input, with how much of it is in stock out of what a batch needs (for example "waiting for Silicon: 0.12 of 0.35 kg"); waiting for power to spare in Daylight, while the battery banks need all the surplus to be full before Night; idle at Night, once the battery banks hold no power to spare; running on and off at Night, with little to spare (see [Power](#power)); or switched off. Two Plants that need the same input start their batches in the order they were commissioned, so the second may wait while the first runs.

**The reserve.** An optics works and a grinding line take the Mission Director's water as coolant, and a Greenhouse drinks it, a batch's worth as each batch starts, like any other input. So that they can't drink it all, the data catalogue keeps a reserve of each life support stock, in Earth Days of what the Mission Director uses: a fortnight, which outlasts a Night and a Lander's round trip. A Plant starts no batch that would take the stock below it, and any other Plants' claims that game-minute come first. It waits, and the Base panel says so, with how much there is, how much it needs and what is kept (for example "waiting for Water: 49.5 of 50 kg, to leave the Mission Director's reserve of 49 kg"). It starts again by itself once there is a batch's worth above the reserve. Below it, the stock goes only to the Mission Director. So a Plant can never kill them, though it can leave them with no more than a fortnight's water, and the water stays there however much the Plants want.

**A Greenhouse** (the Lunar greenhouses Design, past Cold-trap mining) is a Plant that goes on through the Night, and makes nothing to store: it earns Science by rounds of growing, as a telescope does by rounds of observing. The data catalogue says how long a round is and what it pays.

- **Its water** it takes an hour's worth at a time, as any Plant takes a batch's inputs: so what a round has drunk is gone if the round is lost, and it starts no batch that would take water below the [reserve](#plants). It takes its turn with other Plants that want the same water, in the order they were commissioned. The status panel's Water line says what it is using, and how soon what the Plants take will have brought water down to the reserve, Night included.
- **Its power** it draws as a Structure does, not on what is to spare: its pumps in its turn among the other Structures, all through Daylight, and its lamps as a Night load, after the heaters and with the battery banks' reserve kept (see [Power](#power)). So the power figures and forecasts count it, and it gives up its lamps rather than leave anything else without power. While they are on, the dome glows, and lights the ground round it.
- **A round** grows every game-minute it has both, by its performance's share of a game-minute, so more slowly at a low Condition. Once it has its length, its Science is added to the base's and the next begins, with a notification. Each Greenhouse grows its own, so two earn twice what one does, and one can lose its round while another keeps it.
- **A shortage of water or power** loses all that the current round has grown, with a notification, and the Base panel says what it is waiting for. It starts a new round by itself as soon as it has both. So that a base just short of water isn't told so every hour, a round that has grown no more than an hour goes without a notification.
- **Switching it off** gives up the round as its last batch finishes, with a notification, and it then draws no power, so a Greenhouse can't be switched off to save a round through the Night. It starts a new round when switched on.
- **Broken**, it keeps what it has grown for its repair, as a Plant's batch does. Dismantling it loses the round.

It can't kill the Mission Director: it stops at the reserve, and what the Mission Director drinks from there goes on as the forecast says. Nor does it count against self-sufficiency in water, as what any Plant uses doesn't.

**A growing hall** (the Growing halls Design, past Lunar greenhouses and the Optics grinding line) follows all the same rules as a Greenhouse, with numbers of its own in the data catalogue. It is far dearer than the Greenhouses that earn as much, and takes less water for each Science. Its round is longer than a Night, so a Night short of water or power always costs a whole one.

**An automated experiments lab** (the Automated experiments Design, past Precision optics) is a Plant that makes nothing to store either: while it has its power, water and oxygen, the Research Task running advances faster. It follows a Greenhouse's rules for its supplies, with numbers of its own in the data catalogue.

- **Its speed-up**: every game-minute it has all three, the Research Task running advances 1.3 game-minutes more, times its performance, so one runs it at ×2.3 and two at ×3.6, and less at a low Condition. It changes no Research Task: one completes when it has run its length, pays its usual Science, and the next in the queue starts at its usual length. What the labs do beyond a task's length isn't carried over. The queue and its limit are as they were.
- **Its water and oxygen** it takes an hour's worth at a time, never below the [reserve](#plants), and only while a Research Task is running: with none, it starts no batch, draws no power, and the Base panel says it is idle, until one starts.
- **Its power** it draws as a Greenhouse does, in its turn among the Structures by day and as a Night load at Night, keeping the battery banks' reserve (see [Power](#power)). So the forecasts count it, and it gives it up before anything else goes without. A Night of it is more than a large battery bank stores.
- **A shortage** of any of them only stops the speed-up, until it is supplied again: nothing is lost. The Research Tasks screen shows the rate (for example "×2.3"), the time left at it, and how many labs are speeding the task up or short of something; the status panel's Research Task line gives the time left at the rate.
- **Switched off**, it stops once its last batch finishes. **Broken**, it adds nothing until it is repaired.

**An infrared telescope** (the Infrared astronomy Design, past Optical telescopes and Precision optics) is a Plant that earns Science by rounds, as a Greenhouse does, on a standing survey of its own, the Deep infrared survey. The optical telescope and its Night-sky survey are unchanged.

- **At Night only.** Each round is a set time of observing at Night, and pays several times what a round of the Night-sky survey does. Daylight only pauses it: what it has observed waits for the next Night. Each telescope observes its own round, so two earn twice what one does.
- **Its dark radius.** It can't observe while a lit Structure (any with a light radius, such as a Light Mast) that is commissioned and not Broken has any part of its footprint within 80 m of it. It then draws no power and takes no propellant, and the Base panel and its pop-up say why, naming what is in the way. Placing one shows the radius as a pale disc, rings each lit Structure within it, and the hint says whether anything would stop it there. So it is built away from the base, at the end of a long cable. The rule is the data catalogue's `keepClear`: the radius, and the kind of Structure it is kept clear of.
- **Its propellant** cools its camera, boiling off an hour's worth at a time, as any Plant takes a batch's inputs. It never takes the propellant the Lander needs for the Exports set to load: what is to be sold of it, and what the Lander will burn lifting them and anything aboard, until it takes off. So its Science competes with the propellant the base sells.
- **Its power** it draws as a Night load, as the optical telescope does, after the heaters and keeping the battery banks' reserve (see [Power](#power)). A Night of it is more than a large battery bank stores.
- **A shortage of propellant or power, or a light within its dark radius,** loses all the current round has observed, with a notification, and it starts a new round by itself once it can observe again. As with a Greenhouse, a round no more than an hour old goes without a notification. Switching it off gives up the round as its last batch finishes. **Broken**, it keeps what it has observed for its repair.

**Storage limits.** The base's own tanks hold a set amount of oxygen and of water, which the data catalogue gives: 100 kg and 400 kg. They belong to the base rather than a Habitat, as the Mission Director starts aboard the Lander with stock already held and a Habitat can be dismantled. Each commissioned storage tank adds to that. The status panel shows each stock against its limit (for example "Water 399.5 of 400 kg"), with a bar under it for how full that is, which turns to the warning colour with its line. Propellant has a limit too (see below). Water ice, a solid in containers, and the other bulk resources have none.

- A Plant whose only output is a stock with no room for a whole batch, once what the batches under way will make is counted, starts no batch. It waits, using no power or inputs, and the Base panel says so ("waiting for tank space: Water is 399.5 of 400 kg"). It starts again by itself as soon as a batch fits. So the stock rests within a batch of the limit, and little is wasted however long it stays there: it never takes an input for what it can't store. A Plant short of an input says that first, as it couldn't run even with room.
- A Plant with several outputs keeps running as long as one has room, and a Plant that makes Components as well as a stock always does. What doesn't fit of a full stock is lost, so that oxygen can't block metals. Only if all its outputs are limited stocks with no room does it wait.
- A batch finishing into a store that something else has filled since it started finishes all the same, as its inputs are spent. What fits goes in and the rest is lost.
- The Launch manifest warns, but doesn't refuse, if what it brings of a stock is more than there is room for as things stand, with how much there is room for. As the Lander lands, a stock takes what fits, up to its limit, and the rest is lost, with a notification saying how much. It can't be kept aboard, as the Lander flies again. The Launch manifest says this before it is paid for, and stock used during the trip makes room.
- A stock already over its limit, as from a save older than the limit, stays as it is. Nothing takes the excess away: Plants wait until it has fallen, and a landing or a batch adds none of what it brings.

**Propellant's limit.** Each commissioned propellant plant of either size holds 450 kg of propellant, as the data catalogue says, and that and any propellant tanks are what the base can hold. So one Plant holds more than a tripled Return Mass burns, 400 kg, and less than the 600 kg of its ceiling, which takes a tank or a second Plant. The status panel's Bulk resources show it against its limit (for example "Propellant 449.0 of 450 kg"). No recipe that makes propellant makes anything else, so a Plant with no room for a batch always waits, as above, and the Base panel says so ("waiting for tank space: Propellant is 450.0 of 450 kg"). Its line says "holding full" while Plants are keeping it there. No Launch can bring any, so the Launch manifest has nothing to warn of. Burning it and selling it make room like anything else.

- A propellant plant counts from its commissioning until it is Broken or the Robot starts taking it apart, as a tank does, and when it is repaired it counts again.
- Unlike a tank, a Plant that stops counting loses nothing: the limit falls and the stock stays, over it if need be. A Plant is there to make propellant rather than keep it, and nothing else a base can do would insure it against a Micrometeorite striking its only Plant. Plants that are left wait until the stock has fallen enough for a batch.
- A base with no working Plant and no tank has a limit of none. Whatever propellant it has stays, and can be burned or sold, and its status panel line shows the amount alone. Nothing can make any until there is a Plant, and with a Plant there is a limit again.

**Storage tanks** are Structures: from the Bulk supply tanks Design, a water tank and an oxygen tank, the large insulated sphere with a cooler; from the Propellant production Design, a propellant tank, a fat silver capsule with a cooler; and from the Continuous excavation Design, a Regolith bin, an open bay of metal walls. Their Components come by Launch, with nothing in them, and each takes a cable. A tank adds what the data catalogue says to its stock's limit from the moment it is commissioned until it is Broken or the Robot starts taking it apart. Life support resupply stays the way to ship oxygen and water.

- **Its share.** The stock is shared out, in proportion to what each holds, among the base's own tanks and its working storage tanks, as a network's energy is among its battery banks: 200 kg of oxygen with one oxygen tank (100 kg and 300 kg) is 50 kg and 150 kg. Propellant is shared the same way among the working propellant plants and tanks, though only a tank's share is lost as it Breaks. A tank holds no more than it adds, so what a stock has over its limit isn't in it. The Base panel and pop-up say what a tank holds and draws, and the lights on the post beside its model show how full it is.
- **Night.** A tank draws its power only at Night, all through it, to stop what it holds freezing or boiling off. It isn't a Night load: it is supplied with the other Structures, in its turn, and doesn't switch itself off to save power, so the power figures and forecasts count all of it, and a light or a telescope gives way before it does.
- **With no power at Night**, because it isn't on a network with a battery bank, or the battery banks have run out, a tank keeps its place in the limit but loses part of its share every game-minute, at a rate in the data catalogue for each kind: oxygen boils off, and water freezes and splits the seams, more slowly. A notification says so as it starts, and the Base panel says it is losing it, until it has power again or Daylight comes. The oxygen and water forecasts don't count this: they follow what is used, and the notification is the warning.
- **A Broken tank** loses its share at once, as a Broken battery bank loses its stored energy, and the limit falls by what it added. A repair brings back the limit, not what was lost.
- **Dismantling a tank** moves what it holds to the base's other tanks: as the Robot starts taking it apart the limit falls, and only what no longer fits is lost, with a notification. So nothing is lost if there is room left.
- **The Mission Director's reserve.** None of these takes a life support stock below the [reserve](#plants), so a tank can never kill them. Nobody depends on propellant, so no reserve is kept of it: a Broken tank's share is really lost, and all of it if that was all there was. What was over a limit in an older save is taken down only as far as the rules above say, and otherwise stays.

While a stock is full, its forecast says it is "holding full" rather than rising or lasting so long, and no warning comes. It says so when a Plant is waiting for tank space for it, or running with no room for another batch; with no Plant making it, or the only one switched off or Broken, a stock at its limit is simply falling, and the forecast says how long it lasts. A life support stock over its limit, as from an older save, is the exception: nothing is holding it there, so its forecast says it is falling, and by how much.

A Plant with more than one recipe has a recipe list beside it in the Base panel. Choosing another switches it from its next batch, as one it has started finishes with the recipe it was started with; it is a setting, so no Robot does any work for it. The forecasts follow the recipe each Plant runs. So that they never run ahead of the stocks, they count what each running Plant makes a batch late, as it comes only as the batch finishes, and the inputs it has taken for its batch as if it used them evenly through the hour: while production outruns use, the status panel shows a stock rising instead of when it runs out. Plants may stand idle at Night, so the oxygen and water forecasts count on them making nothing through it: in Daylight they follow the current rate to nightfall, then what the Mission Director uses through the Night, and so on through the Lunations ahead, and at Night they look as far as the next Daylight. A stock that is rising but won't last the Night ahead shows when it will run out, and its warnings come before Night falls; one that will last it gets none. The water forecast counts what running Plants use as well, in how fast it is changing and when it will run out: they go on taking it down to the reserve, through a Night too as they may have the power, then stop, and the forecast follows what the Mission Director uses from there. So the warnings, which start at less than the reserve lasts, come only once the reserve itself is running down, not as Plants take what is above it. A Plant that makes the stock counts as making none, by Night as by day, while it is short of what it makes it from: the forecast then falls at what is used, without waiting for the next Daylight to show it, so that the warnings come while there is time to send for more. The status panel's Water line says which Plants are using it and how much a day, and which are waiting at the reserve.

"Switch off" beside a Plant in the Base panel stops it starting batches, to keep an input it would use, or to leave its share of the spare Daylight power to the Plants after it; "Switch on" lets it start them again from the next game-minute. Both are settings, so no Robot does any work for them. A batch it has started when switched off finishes, as its inputs are already taken, and the Base panel says it is its last. The forecasts count a switched-off Plant as making and using nothing from the moment it is switched off, and the oxygen and water lines in the status panel name any that would make them. Standing idle, it wears no further by Dust, but Micrometeorites can still strike it and the Robot still maintains it, so that it is ready to switch back on.

A Workshop's spare Components join the base's stock as each batch finishes, and Maintenance uses them like any others. Running through the Daylight, it makes a little more than a base of about a dozen Structures uses in a Lunation, on most of the metals such a base has to spare beside its circuit board plant, so once it runs the base can stop spending Payload Mass on spares, and a Launch that would have brought only those can bring nothing and pay no fee, but has little metal left over for anything else. A larger base needs a second one. Switch it off once there are enough in stock to keep the metals.

An array works makes what a solar array is built from, so that more power needs no Launch, at the cost of silicon and metals that would otherwise have been sold. It can make far more than a base uses: switch it off once there are enough.

An extraction plant, a vacuum fabrication plant and a circuit board plant are balanced to run together on what two solar arrays can spare. One extraction plant can't keep the Mission Director in oxygen and feed a vacuum fabrication plant at once, so switch its recipe as the stocks need, or ship a second.

### Self-sufficiency

The status panel shows whether the base is self-sufficient in oxygen and in water: whether, over the last Lunation, its Plants made at least as much as the Mission Director used, judged at regular intervals. Because it looks back a whole Lunation, it holds through a Night when the Plants stand idle. What Plants use of a stock doesn't count: the question is whether the base could keep the Mission Director alive without Earth, and with the reserve, Plants can't take that away from them. So a base can be self-sufficient in water while its grinding line waits for more, and more water has to be made to run it than to keep the title. Being full doesn't lose it, for oxygen and water alike, nor the Milestones that ask for it: a game-minute a Plant spends waiting for tank space counts as if it had made what the Mission Director used in that minute, Night included, up to what it makes when it runs, so a whole Lunation of holding full comes to 100%. All that a Plant with several outputs makes counts, including what is lost for want of room. A Plant short of its inputs, Broken or switched off isn't waiting for tank space, so it gets no such credit, however full the store. It can't hold before a whole Lunation has passed since the Campaign began (or since an older save was loaded), or while the Mission Director lives aboard the Lander. Gaining and losing it are notifications.

Once the base has a Plant that makes spare Components, it shows the same for them: whether, over the last Lunation, its Plants made at least as many as Maintenance and the Robots' repairs of themselves used. What a Launch brings doesn't count.

## Research and the Tech Tree

**Research Tasks** earn Science. The Research Tasks screen shows the one running, with its progress, those queued behind it, the observing programme (see below), and every other with its length, the Science it pays and the Designs that Science would bring within reach (or, if too few, how far short of the cheapest it leaves you), below what Science buys next; short ones pay more for each hour, long ones run unattended. Only one runs at a time, and each can be completed only once. While one runs, up to three more can wait in the queue: when the one running completes, the first in the queue starts by itself, and a notification says so after the one saying it completed, which "Skip to next event" stops at. A task can be queued only if it could start now, so not while its Design is locked, and not twice. A queued one can be removed at any time, which loses nothing as it has done no work; the one running can't be cancelled. The status panel's Research section shows the queue in order, and it is saved with the Campaign. A Research Task costs only game time: it needs no Robot, power or Components, and carries on through Night and everything else. Many need a Design unlocked first and are hidden until then. They range from hours to Lunations, and the Designs deeper in the Tech Tree cost the most, so research carries on long after the base is self-sufficient.

**The observing programme** is a standing Research Task that the base's telescopes (the Optical telescopes Design) carry out, alongside the one the player has started, for as long as the base has one. It works a round at a time: every game-minute of Night, each commissioned telescope that has power to spare and isn't Broken observes, and once the telescopes have observed a round's length between them, the round is complete, its Science is added, a notification says so, and the next round starts. Two telescopes get through a round twice as fast, and one at a low Condition more slowly. Nothing is observed in Daylight, without power or while Broken, and the part of a round already observed waits, even through Daylight, for the next game-minute a telescope observes. A telescope draws power only while it observes, so nothing in Daylight. Like a light, it is supplied at Night after the heaters, and switches itself off to save power once its network's battery banks hold only enough for everything else through the rest of the Night and the same reserve the lights keep (see [Power](#power)), so it never leaves the Habitat or a heater without power; the Base panel then shows it not observing, to save power. Build battery banks enough for it if it is to observe all Night. The status panel's Research section shows the programme once the base has a telescope, with how long its round has left while a telescope observes or why none is, and how far the round has got beside the folded section's title; and the Base panel shows each telescope that is observing.

**A Greenhouse's rounds of growing** earn Science too, by the same path, once it is commissioned (see [Plants](#plants)), and so do a growing hall's. The last Design costs more than Research Tasks pay, so a base needs them to reach it, and more of them reach it sooner.

**Science earned a day.** Once the base first earns any, the status panel's Research section says how much Science it earns an Earth Day as things stand, such as "+3.0 a day", with where it comes from on hovering over the line. Science comes a round at a time, so this is a forecast, not a count: each round's Science spread over the time the round takes at the pace it is worked now, for the Research Task running (faster with automated experiments labs), the observing programme, the infrared and radio surveys, and the rounds of growing. The observing programme and the infrared survey work only at Night, so they count for the Night's share of a Lunation, in Daylight too, which keeps the figure from swinging between the two; in Daylight every telescope that isn't Broken or otherwise stopped is taken to observe the Night ahead. A round lost to a shortage earns nothing, which the figure doesn't foresee.

**Unspent Science.** With nothing queued, the Research section's Queued line says when the base's Science would unlock a Design, by name if there is one and how many otherwise, such as "Science for 3 Designs", picked out in the accent colour. Research Tasks cost no Science, so only the Tech Tree counts.

**An automated experiments lab** makes the Research Task running advance faster while it is supplied, a game-minute more for every game-minute for each lab (see [Plants](#plants)), so a base can shorten the long Research Tasks of Act 4 by building out its power and life support.

**An infrared telescope** earns Science by rounds of its own survey at Night, far from the base's lights and cooled by its propellant (see [Plants](#plants)), so a late base can turn Funding, propellant and power into Science.

**A radio telescope array** (the Radio astronomy Design, past Optical telescopes and Precision optics) earns Science by rounds of its own standing survey, the Radio sky survey, as a Greenhouse does. It isn't a Plant: it takes nothing from the base's stocks.

- **Through Daylight and Night.** Each round is a set time of working, and the array works whenever it has power and no Plant is within its quiet radius. Each array works its own round, so two earn twice what one does.
- **Its antenna fields.** It is built with one antenna field, and each round's work goes as fast as its fields allow: the Base panel and its pop-up show how many it has, how many add nothing, and what it earns a Lunation at that rate. An **antenna field** (the same Design) is a Structure of its own, built through Construction like any other with its own Components (sixteen antenna tiles and a field receiver), and it may be placed only beside a commissioned radio telescope array that isn't to be dismantled: its footprint within 10 m of the array's, or of an antenna field's, commissioned or being built, that joins one. Anywhere else the build menu refuses it and says why. It joins that array (the nearest, if it is beside two), and once commissioned adds a whole field's work to the array's round, as much again as the array's own field, at the field's own Condition, while it has power: so each field earns as much Science as the array did alone. A Broken field, or one without power, adds nothing until it is repaired or has power again, but loses the array no round. A field draws a small steady power, by day and at Night, and isn't a Plant. Its pop-up says whether it is adding to its array.
- **Dismantling.** Dismantling an antenna field slows its array again, once the Robot starts taking it apart. The array itself can't be dismantled while an antenna field joins it, commissioned or being built: the game refuses and says to dismantle the fields first (or cancel one being built).
- **Its quiet radius.** It can't work while a Plant that is commissioned, not Broken and not switched off has any part of its footprint within 120 m of it, or of any commissioned antenna field joining it, as the Plants' machinery drowns the sky's faint signals: the quiet radius is the whole array's, its fields included. The Base panel, its pop-up and the status panel say why, naming what is in the way. Placing one, or an antenna field, shows the radius as a pale disc, rings each Plant within it, and the hint says whether anything would stop it there. So it is built away from the base, at the end of a long cable. Another array isn't a Plant, so arrays never stop each other. It is the same `keepClear` rule as an infrared telescope's dark radius, kept clear of Plants rather than lights.
- **Its power** is a steady draw, by day and at Night, which it takes in its turn among the Structures (see [Power](#power)), and goes on drawing while a Plant stands too near. A Night of it is a little over half of what a large battery bank stores.
- **A shortage of power, or a Plant within its quiet radius,** loses all the current round has done, with a notification, and it starts a new round by itself once it can work again. A round no more than an hour old goes without a notification. **Broken**, it keeps what it has done for its repair.

**The Tech Tree** spends Science on Designs. Its screen has a tab for each category (Power, Construction, Protection, Life Support, Industry and Mobility), with each Design's cost, what unlocking it brings, the Designs it needs first and whether it can be unlocked now. Everything available at the start belongs to a starting Design.

Unlocking a Design is permission, not the thing itself: its items appear on the Launch manifest and in the build menu, but still have to be shipped and built. Nothing already in stock, on its way or planned is affected, and Structures already built keep their old behaviour.

**Solar array upgrades add up.** The build menu has one solar array. What it turns out to be comes from the Components it is built from: a sealed mast wears down more slowly, screened panels gather Dust coating more slowly, thin-film panels generate 15 kW rather than 10, and a prefabricated pack, with whatever mast and panels were on sale when it was made, goes up in under half the hours and fails Commissioning less often. There is no magic: a technology changes nothing that already exists. Arrays already built stay as they were built, and so do Components at the base, or on a Lander. Instead the Launch manifest takes the old Component off sale, leaving one mast and one panel, or once arrays are prefabricated one pack, the best the unlocked Designs allow. A replaced Component still on a manifest not yet paid for comes off it, with a notification; one on a Lander that has left Earth comes as paid for. A new array is built from what the base has, the best first, and whatever else it needs is bought new, so the array works' plain Components still go into plain arrays. Until the Robot takes its Components, a planned array can change kind, so it never waits for one no longer made, and it takes a whole set in stock rather than wait for another. A lone mast or panel with nothing on sale to match it stays in stock unused. The build menu says what the next one will be.

## Saving

"Save" in the top bar downloads the Campaign as a `.moonbase` file named after the game clock, and "Load" carries on from a file you choose, in place of the Campaign you're playing. The game-over screen has both. Nothing is kept in the browser, so a Campaign you haven't saved is lost when you close or reload the page (ADR-0006). A file that isn't a Moon Base save, is damaged, or comes from a newer version of the game is refused, and the Campaign you're playing carries on.
