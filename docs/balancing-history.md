# Balancing history

What the long-game play guide played at each data catalogue version, and what the maintainer's playtests found, moved here from [`docs/balancing.md`](balancing.md) unedited, apart from links into it. Each section names the part of `docs/balancing.md` it came from, which "above" and "below" in it refer to. `docs/balancing.md` keeps the targets, the decisions behind the numbers and the latest play; each figure here is from the version its section names, so measure again before relying on one. The last section came from [`docs/adding-content.md`](adding-content.md) instead.

## Water as coolant, at version 67

The long-game play guide, at data catalogue version 67, plays no differently for it: it still builds the same one water-processing plant and cold-trap ice line, and brings 100 kg of water by Launch, at $154M with the fee, whenever it has under 120 kg. It completes with every pacing target met, and nothing in Acts 1 to 3 moves: the Funding is still $14,524M on Earth Day 1,500, precision optics are first sold on 1,688, the last Design is unlocked on 2,381 and research ends on 2,981. In Act 4 it sold 2,349 kg of precision optics, 10,361 kg of circuit boards and 3,610 kg of propellant, $49,509M over 153 trips, and ends with $42,805M against the $91,680M it had before. 65 of the 126 Launches after the first optics sold were sent, at $154M, with water and nothing else of note. Its water reaches its lowest, 14.8 kg, on Earth Day 2,908.7: the Plants stop at 49 kg at dusk, and the Mission Director drinks 33 kg of that through the Night, which leaves about 4 Earth Days of use at dawn. No warning comes, as the forecast at Night looks only as far as the next Daylight, and the stock lasts it. The Mission Director moved into the Buried Habitat on Earth Day 1,591.4 at 561.7 mSv and ended at 588.9 mSv, 0.0194 mSv an Earth Day, with the limit forecast for Earth Day 3,738.

So they prove the play, not the worth: what they are worth is for a base that makes more water than it uses by day, such as one with a Greenhouse to carry through the Night (#186).

**The playtest (#189).** The maintainer played Acts 3 and 4 with the water economy in the game, found that it worked well and kept every starting number, so nothing in the catalogue changed. The figures above are all still the guide's, not the maintainer's.

Funding, water, and knowing what to aim for were all fine: what came next was usually the equipment not yet bought. Radiation was fine too, but it needed watching, as at 20x the Earth Days go fast. Their look-and-feel notes don't touch the balance, so they go elsewhere. ## Plants in Daylight, at version 80

From [Plants in Daylight while the battery banks charge, at version 80 (#247)](balancing.md#the-larger-changes-and-why).

**Plants in Daylight while the battery banks charge, at version 80 (#247).** A Plant in Daylight used to run only on what the battery banks couldn't store, so a base built to run its Plants through the Night stood idle every morning until its banks were full again, however much surplus it had. Now it runs whenever, with it running, the banks would still be full 24 hours before Night falls (`daylightChargeMarginHours`), decided afresh each game-minute from what is generated and drawn. The Plants get no more energy over a Daylight than the banks don't need, but none of it goes to waste while the banks charge, so a base short of power makes more. A base with power to spare, as the long-game base has from its concentrator tower on, fills its banks within a day of dawn either way.

**What the guide played, at version 80.** The richer early game moves everything from before the Buried Habitat, which the guide moved into on Earth Day 1,591.1, against 1,591.6 at version 78. Three changes to the guide came with it. It plans a Buried Habitat only with 16 Earth Days of Daylight left, not 12: building one took about 10.6 and dismantling the old Habitat about 4.9, and with just over 12 left the dismantling ran into the Night, with both Habitats drawing, and the Mission Director died near its end. It builds a third Workshop with the Greenhouse, as with two the stock of spare Components ran out from about Earth Day 2,050 until the growing hall's, now its fourth, came. And its limit on a purchase making no progress counts the water rising towards what it holds back to stock the Large-scale Crew Habitat, as with more Funding it planned the habitat on Earth Day 2,427, about 400 Earth Days before the water was there. Every pacing target is met.

## Prospecting, the Regolith top-up and the digger, at versions 82 and 83

From [Prospecting before the Plants](balancing.md#the-larger-changes-and-why), [Topping up the Regolith before Night](balancing.md#the-larger-changes-and-why) and [A bucket-wheel digger and Regolith bins](balancing.md#the-larger-changes-and-why).

**Prospecting before the Plants, at version 81 (#249).** Prospecting Expeditions used to cost 80 Science past Parts machining, 350 in with what it needed, so it came long after the Plants made metals and silicon in quantity, and a Robot and the Rover six Earth Days away for 30 kg had nothing to do. It now costs 30 past Robots, 50 in all. In the reference Campaign the first Research Task starts on Earth Day 15.8 and the second Launch, with the first extraction plant, lands about 12 Earth Days later: the Research Tasks that need no Design pay 100 Science in their first 10 Earth Days back to back, enough for Water-wall shielding's 40 and these 50, so a player can send for metals or silicon before the Plants run. It sits past Robots because an Expedition takes a Robot away for six Earth Days, so it comes with the Design that lets a base ship a second. That Robot comes by Launch, on the second at the soonest, so a base that sends one before it lands works without a Robot for those days: the player's trade-off. What it brings is unchanged, below one extraction plant's rate, so it bridges a base to its Plants and then only tops it up. The Deposit assay survey keeps its 80 Science, so the Science to spare over the whole Tech Tree is 50 more than before, and the survey is offered much earlier. The long-game play guide unlocks the cheapest Design it can, so it now buys Prospecting Expeditions in its first weeks, which moves the order of its early unlocks and Research Tasks; it still sends no Prospecting. Its figures at version 82, with the Regolith top-up below, are under [what the guide played at versions 82 and 83](#prospecting-the-regolith-top-up-and-the-digger-at-versions-82-and-83). The reference Campaign's timeline is unchanged, as it unlocks only Water-wall shielding.

**Topping up the Regolith before Night, at version 82 (#250).** The excavator is parked through the Night, 228 hours with no digging, and the stock went into it anywhere between the refill level, 1,000 kg, and full, 2,000 kg, as the Daylight's last dig had left it. Plants running on stored power at Night could empty that in days, and the pile then sat near empty until dawn with the Robots idle. So in the 30 hours before parking starts (`topUpHoursBeforeParking`) digging also starts below 1,950 kg (`topUpBelowKilograms`). From just above the refill level a top-up is some 20 hours' digging at full Condition, so 30 leave time for the drive and a worn excavator; starting only an hour's digging short of full keeps a Plant from having a Robot out every few kilograms. That is up to 1,000 kg more going into each Night, for up to 20 more hours of the excavator's digging, about 8 percentage points of its Condition, a Lunation. ## Plants in whole-number ratios, at version 92

From [Plants in whole-number ratios](balancing.md#the-larger-changes-and-why).

**Plants in whole-number ratios, at version 92 (#339).** A player who builds Plants in the right numbers should gather no silicon or wafers. Before version 92 none of the ratios were whole: an extraction plant made 1.14 times the silicon a vacuum fabrication plant took, which made 1.2 times the wafers a circuit board plant took, and a large extraction plant made 2.44 times the silicon an integrated fabrication plant took. Only the Plants that take things in moved, so oxygen, power and the producers' numbers are unchanged, and each kept roughly its yield. A vacuum fabrication plant takes 0.4 kg of silicon an hour for 0.34 kg of wafers (from 0.35 for 0.3). A circuit board plant takes 0.34 kg of wafers and 0.3 kg of metals for 0.5 kg of circuit boards (from 0.25 and 0.25 for 0.4). An integrated fabrication plant takes 0.55 kg of silicon on either recipe, with 0.4 kg of metals for 0.6 kg of circuit boards (from 0.45 and 0.3 for 0.5) or alone for 0.47 kg of wafers (from 0.7 for 0.6). So an extraction plant feeds one vacuum fabrication plant and one circuit board plant, with half its metals left over for a Workshop, which takes a quarter of them, or to sell, and a large extraction plant feeds two integrated fabrication plants with half its metals left over. Metals are left over because they are an Export, and because closing them as well would mean circuit board plants taking twice the metals. The integrated fabrication plant's circuit boards stop at 0.6 kg an hour, a fifth more than a circuit board plant's rather than a quarter, because about 0.61 would fill the Return Mass and a Plant's rate has to stay what limits circuit boards (see Act 2 above). Making more circuit boards from the same Plants raises income in Acts 1 and 2. `test/catalogue-plant-ratios.test.ts` checks the ratios, and CI's long-game play measures what that does to the timeline.

## The larger changes, and why, up to version 93

From [The larger changes, and why](balancing.md#the-larger-changes-and-why), in that order: each passage as it stood before the figures it had replaced were taken out of it. Each still gives the reason for a number the catalogue holds.

**Battery banks.** A Night lasts 228 hours. Through it the Habitat draws 274 kWh (1.2 kW), each solar array 11 kWh, and the heaters of the excavator, the regolith printer and the Rover, which the Robot plugs in before every Night, 137 kWh between them. A battery bank stores 200 kWh, about what a small electric car carries, and its heaters draw 0.07 kW, 16 kWh through a Night. The starting stock holds three battery banks, 600 kWh, which carry the Habitat, two solar arrays and all the heaters through a Night, about 480 kWh, with over 100 kWh to spare; two would not. The large battery bank stays the better buy: 800 kWh for 0.2 kW of heaters, less mass and money for each kWh. (A battery bank used to store 60 kWh, and its heaters to draw 0.25 kW, 57 of those 60 through a Night, so no number of battery banks could carry anything else through one.)

**The Radiation Dose.** Regolith shielding cuts the dose rate to a twentieth, 1.5 mSv an Earth Day. With Solar Particle Events on top it rises about 2 mSv an Earth Day, so the 600 mSv limit comes in about nine Lunations: the Hazard still bites over the long run. Water-wall shielding cuts it to a sixth of that, 0.25 mSv an Earth Day (a tenth, 0.15, before version 93: see The water wall weakened, below). In the reference Campaign the Research Tasks pay for it by Earth Day 20, the third Launch brings its bladders, and it is filled once there is 300 kg of water to spare beyond a fortnight's drinking water, on Earth Day 96.0. From then on the limit is about 2.6 years away, around Earth Day 1,110 (over four years before version 93); without it, under Regolith shielding alone, it would come about five months after the end. The Radiation Dose never falls, so what that first Lunation or two takes is taken for good, and the water wall decides whether there is any of the limit left by the time a Buried Habitat can be had. A base that skips the wall reaches the limit around Earth Day 260, well before the around Earth Day 335 that it could afford a Buried Habitat even if it bought nothing else; a base behind the wall has about 850 Earth Days more to save up (about 1,400 before version 93).

**Research that lasts.** The Research Tasks used to take 63 Earth Days back to back and pay 631 Science, against a Tech Tree costing 625, so a player who kept one running had every Design by about Earth Day 80, half-way through the reference Campaign. Twelve longer tasks, from 8 Earth Days to two Lunations and paying about 0.3 Science an hour (the earlier ones pay about 0.4), brought that to about 315 Earth Days and 2435 Science at the time. Seven of them are offered once a Design that had none of its own is unlocked. The Designs' costs now rise with their depth in the tree. Those the early game leans on cost what they did (Robots, Prefabricated and Dust-sealed solar arrays, Large battery banks and Water-wall shielding) or a little more, a few Earth Days of research each. The rest of the first rank cost 60–150, and those past it, at the time, 150–340, from a few weeks to a Lunation and a half of research each. The whole tree then cost 2225: the Research Tasks have to pay for it even when the last Design unlocked is Integrated fabrication, whose own task's 148 Science then has nothing left to buy. The reference Campaign unlocks only Water-wall shielding, at the same 40 Science and the same time, so its timeline is unchanged.

**The Workshop.** The long-game base, with its 13 Structures, used 16.5 spare Components a Lunation between Earth Days 100 and 1,650 (from 8 to 25), as the long-game play guide showed before there was a Workshop. Metals piled up beside its circuit board plant at about 78 kg a Lunation. A Plant's Components come in whole numbers, so a Batch of a game-hour would make at least one an hour: 480 in a Daylight, about thirty times the use, and all the metals gone in days. So a Workshop's recipe has a Batch of its own length, a whole Earth Day (`batchHours`), and makes one spare Component in it: 20 in a Daylight, a little more than the base uses, leaving room for the Workshop's own wear (about one spare Component a Lunation) and the worse Lunations. Each takes 3.6 kg of metals, 72 kg a Lunation running through the Daylight: most of what the base has to spare, so that Act 2's Export of metals has to choose between selling them and running a Workshop. Against a Launch's spare Components, 20 kg and $1M each, it saves the base up to 400 kg of Payload Mass and $20M a Lunation, and its own 600 kg pays back in about a Lunation and a half; `npm run pacing-report` works this out. It costs $1000M, repriced from its first $40M for Act 2 (see Act 2: Funding below), which those spares alone would never pay back: what does is the Launch fee saved once a Launch brings nothing, about $170M a Lunation. It draws 2 kW, which the long-game base's two solar arrays have to spare beside its other Plants at 13 kW, except for short spells when Dust and wear have cut them. Its Design, Parts machining, costs 150 Science, in the second rank of the tree past Molten regolith electrolysis. So that the Research Tasks still pay for the whole tree even when Integrated fabrication is the last Design unlocked, the Machined part wear trial pays 108 Science of it, over 14 Earth Days: at the time that made them 2543 Science against the Designs' 2375, a margin of 20 once Integrated fabrication's own task has nothing left to buy. The reference Campaign never unlocks it, so its timeline is unchanged. One Workshop was enough for the long-game base as it stood, but not once Act 2 had grown it: see below.

**The Buried Habitat.** ADR-0008 chose it as the way past the Radiation Dose limit. Behind a water wall the dose rises 0.15 mSv an Earth Day and, with Solar Particle Events, 0.197 in the long-game play guide: they multiply it 1.32 times, against the 1.33 that their average length and spacing work out at. So that 30 mSv lasts 1,400 Earth Days, the rate with Solar Particle Events has to be at most 0.02, so the usual rate is 0.015, a tenth of a water wall's. In the long-game play guide it came to 0.0196 mSv an Earth Day from moving in on Earth Day 1,593 at 558.0 mSv to Earth Day 3,000 at 585.5 mSv, with the game's forecast then putting the limit on Earth Day 3,964. A base that doesn't build one is unchanged: the same rates, and the same limit, put at about Earth Day 1,790 at the time (see [Targets](balancing.md#targets) for what has been measured). Its eight vault sections and deep life support core cost $4,450M and weigh 4,900 kg, and with a Habitat's $60M, 400 kg airlock come to three Launches' worth, $450M of fees: $4,960M in all, as the long-game play guide paid (Launches on Earth Days 1,537, 1,548 and 1,569). A section at $500M is the most any Launch item can cost, so that a Launch with one in it could still be paid for from the Programme Grant, were there nothing else on it. That is about what 17 Launches of circuit boards sell for ($300M a Launch, with no fee on one that brings nothing), 255 Earth Days of what a base earns at a trip every 15.4 Earth Days, and about a quarter of the $18,080M the long-game base held on Earth Day 1,530, even after Act 2's purchases. It held $22,400M before them, and Act 3 is to bring more to buy. It takes 120 hours of the excavator's Site Preparation, against no more than 9 for anything else: the 5 Earth Days and about half the excavator's Condition keep it from being built in a moment without leaving the excavator Broken part-way. Its Design costs 400 Science. The Buried vault dose survey pays that, over two Lunations, needing Water-wall shielding: at the time that made the Research Tasks 2943 Science against the Designs' 2775, the same margin of 20 once Integrated fabrication's own task has nothing left to buy, and 389.5 Earth Days back to back. The reference Campaign never gets that far, so its timeline is unchanged. Moving out of a water-walled Habitat leaves it drawing 1.2 kW, and on the long-game base that is too much to keep: its battery banks can't carry both Habitats through a Night, and a first attempt that added a large battery bank and kept both fell to no spare Components by Earth Day 1,700 and died on Earth Day 1,789 once the solar arrays wore out, as the Workshop is last in line for power. So the guide builds the Buried Habitat early in a Daylight, and has the old Habitat, its water wall and its shielding dismantled before Night falls. That is a choice for a player too, not a rule. The figures in this passage are from before version 93, which moved the Buried Habitat into Act 3: see The water wall weakened, below.

**Act 2: what limits each Export.** A circuit board plant makes 240 kg in a Daylight (192 kg before data catalogue version 92) against the 295 kg that a round trip every 10 Earth Days can carry away in a Lunation, and in the long game before Act 2 the Lander sold a full Return Mass of them twice a Lunation, waiting on the Plant each time. So a Plant's rate limits them. Metals had to hit something else, and it can't be the Plant: an extraction plant already makes 288 kg a Daylight, for $75M. So they hit the Return Mass. At $1 a kilogram a full Return Mass sells for $100M, under the Launch fee, and they only earn by filling what circuit boards leave: about 36 kg a trip, once the Lander goes every round trip rather than waits. Any dearer and an extraction plant alone would out-earn a vacuum fabrication plant by too much, and Act 1 would skip the wafers. In Earth Days 350 to 900 the long-game play guide sold 3,534 kg of circuit boards and 1,566 kg of metals over 51 trips, $12,168M, of which metals were 13%. That brings the base up against the Return Mass, which is what Act 3 opens with. A magnetic separator and a bucket-wheel digger were tried on paper, to make Regolith the limit, and cut: a second extraction plant out-made the separator at a twelfth of its price, so neither would have changed a decision.

**Act 2: Science.** Nine Research Tasks add 495.5 Earth Days, and 3,095 Science, paying less for each hour the longer they run, down to about 0.25. None can be started in the reference Campaign: each needs a Design it never unlocks, or runs longer than the longest it gets to. Four Designs cost 500, 600, 800 and 1,050 Science, so at the time the Research Tasks paid 6,038 against the Designs' 5,725, a margin of 68 once Cold-trap mining's own task has nothing left to buy. Played, the last Design is unlocked on Earth Day 864, and the last Research Task completed on 901.

**Act 2: Funding.** Everything the reference Campaign buys has to stay as cheap as it is, or its timeline changes, so all Funding can do is come to matter in what is bought later. That is a Workshop, repriced from $40M to $1000M; an array works at $1500M; a concentrator tower at $2000M; a thermal store at $2000M; and a cold-trap ice line at $3000M: $9,500M, 70% of the $13,620M the pacing report has Exports earning over the Act, with one of every Launch item now taking 42 Launches' profit. No item costs more than $500M. In the long-game play guide, which buys two Workshops and leaves out the thermal store, the Funding stays around $1,000M from Earth Day 400 to 600, with a low of $460M on Earth Day 572. It is then Science that decides when the next purchase comes, and the Funding climbs: $3,084M on Earth Day 700, $4,120M on 900, $18,080M on 1,530, where it was $22,407M before Act 2. So Funding bites for about the first half of the Act, and no further. At data catalogue version 65 the long game also ends on Earth Day 3,000 with more than it did, $45,418M against $42,625M, as the extra trips earn more than Act 2 costs: the rest of the Campaign needs Acts 3 and 4 to give it something to spend on.

**Act 3: propellant.** Propellant is liquid oxygen, a bulk resource of its own rather than the Mission Director's oxygen, so that Export and the consistency rules didn't have to change and selling it can't suffocate anyone. The Lander carries 100 kg on what Earth gives it, and a kilogram more for every 2 kg burned, up to 400 kg. So 2 kg of propellant, worth $2.40M sold, must lift more than that: circuit boards at $3M a kilogram are worth it, metals at $1M aren't. That is the Act's choice, and at $1.20M propellant takes metals' place in what the boards leave of the first 100 kg. A propellant plant makes 480 kg in a Daylight for 15 kg of Regolith an hour; a large one, 1,440 kg for 35 kg an hour, which keeps the excavator busy. In the long-game play guide, one was enough to fly 300 kg once, on Earth Day 1,014, 21 days after the first propellant.

**Act 4: precision optics.** They sell for $6M a kilogram, twice circuit boards. An optics works makes 96 kg in a Daylight, a grinding line 240 kg. At data catalogue version 66, at $9M the guide ended with $118,875M. At $6M it still ended with $91,680M, before they took water: see below.

Lunar greenhouses costs 200 Science, needs Cold-trap mining, and comes with the Closed-loop crop trial, 14 Earth Days for 130 Science. The Science left when the last Design is unlocked was 73 before it, in the catalogue test that plays the tree on Research Tasks alone, so the task has to pay at least what is over that, and it can be no longer than the 19 Earth Days to spare before Earth Day 3,000. Any dearer and the task would pay far above the rate of any other: 600 Science, tried first, wanted 527 from those 14 days. As the cheapest Design left once Cold-trap mining is unlocked, it comes before Propellant production. That moves no date of Act 1 or Act 2, as it needs the last of Act 2's Designs, and the first propellant is still made on Earth Day 993.

**Why a vault cistern changes a decision.** It lifts the Act's own limit again: the limit stands about Earth Day 3,570 behind a Buried Habitat at version 72, and thousands of Earth Days off with one. At version 75 the guide's base on Earth Day 2,850, at 593.4 mSv, has it forecast for about Earth Day 3,290, some 4 mSv higher than at version 72 for reasons not traced, so the cistern now matters to a Campaign played about 300 Earth Days past the end, not 570. A Campaign that goes on past Earth Day 3,000 is the only one that needs it, so a player deciding to stop at 3,000 has no use for it: that is the choice. What it costs is the point. Its water has to be in stock all at once, as a water wall's is, and 2,000 kg is more than the base's own tanks and one water tank hold, so it takes a second tank and a stretch with the Greenhouses or the optics going without: 400 kg of precision optics, $2,400M, or about four rounds of a growing hall. Then there is its 9,000 Science, which no base reaches on Research Tasks alone. It reuses the water wall's rules, with one rate in the data catalogue (`cisternedBuriedHabitat`) and a rule for where it stands. The Tech Tree's other tests still hold: the Research Tasks pay for all but it.

**Why they stand where they do.** Both come past the Optics grinding line, so no date before Earth Day 1,994.7 can move: the guide unlocks the cheapest Design it can, and 800 Science would otherwise come before Large propellant production. Research was already full to Earth Day 3,000, and the guide fails if a task is left undone, so the Hall yield trial's 30 Earth Days come out of the longest task. That is why no task is offered by the Vault cistern: it would pay Science with nothing left to buy.

**Why it stands where it does.** Needing Growing halls keeps it until about Earth Day 2,000 at the soonest, and 2,180 for the guide: with no Design behind it, a base with the Funding of Earth Day 1,800 could have it built well before the end, and the last hours would ask nothing again. It costs only 100 Science, as research is full to Earth Day 3,000 and the tree's other tests hold with it: the Research Tasks still pay for all but the vault cistern. The Design's rules want a Design beyond the starting ones to cost some Science. It is a Life Support Design, as a Habitat is, so it takes the Hazards in full, and its Site Preparation is a day's grading, as the Buried Habitat keeps by far the longest dig. Its stocking reuses the water wall's rule, with a list of stocks in the data catalogue (`stockedWith`), and no saved state.

A first play bought one water tank too few, so the base could hold 2,400 kg of water, less than the 2,549 kg stocking takes with the reserve: the Work panel said so, and the habitat was stocked only once the vault cistern's tank came. The habitat's purchase spans many Launches, so the guide's limit on a purchase making no progress counts each Component that comes as progress (see [the long-game play guide](reference-campaign.md#when-it-fails)); before that, a first play gave up on Earth Day 2,621, one core short. A person has yet to play it: whether saving up for it and stocking it feel like a purpose or a wait, and whether the celebration lands, is for the maintainer's play.

## Still to watch, up to version 93

From [Still to watch](balancing.md#still-to-watch).

- **Funding in the late game.** See above. It is the Campaign's largest open balance problem. The Greenhouse chain took $28,053M of it in the guide at version 72, and left it idle again by the end (see [the balancing history](#the-greenhouse-chain-at-version-72)). At version 76 the Large-scale Crew Habitat takes most of the rest, leaving about $5,400M idle at the end, and at version 78 about $7,500M. At version 88 the late science's Components keep it under $4,000M for much of Act 4, but the precision optics they let the base sell leave $19,859M at the end ([the play](balancing.md#the-late-science-in-the-long-game)).

- **Spare Components.** Dust wears everything down steadily, so a growing base uses spare Components fast: the reference Campaign keeps 30 in stock, topped up by every Launch, until a Workshop takes that over. With fewer, Maintenance waits for spares, solar arrays wear down to Broken, and a Night can then empty the battery banks. With one Workshop the long-game play guide's stock fell to 3 on about Earth Day 2,685 and recovered, for a cause never found; with two and a concentrator tower it no longer does, and its low after Earth Day 2,500 was 25. It has since been less comfortable: with a Greenhouse's second Robot and more Structures its low was 12 at data catalogue version 70, and 2 at version 71, on about Earth Day 2,980, with no trace yet of why. At version 72 it has a third Workshop: with two, a growing hall and two ice lines, in a run also short of water, it stayed at about none from Earth Day 2,330. With three, and all the chain's Structures, its low was 7. Its low was 11 at version 77, and 6 at version 78, in the Lunation to Earth Day 2,596, as the Large-scale Crew Habitat's parts came. At version 80, with Plants running as the battery banks charge, the base grew sooner and two Workshops ran out from about Earth Day 2,050, so the guide builds a third with the Greenhouse and a fourth with the growing hall; its low after Act 2 was 17.

## The catalogue's version, up to version 66

From [The catalogue's version](adding-content.md#the-catalogues-version) in `docs/adding-content.md`, where "all three above" are the commits that added a Workshop (`5a81a54`), a Buried Habitat (`2b5bf20`) and Act 2 (`ce8bc96`).

The rule comes from what the history showed when it was written, at version 66, reached one step at a time:

- Each ticket that adds content raises it by one in the commit that changes the catalogue, as all three above did.
- So do changes to numbers or to the words alone: `b5ca87a` changed one Milestone's hint, and `79fe87b` was for descriptions.
- A change to code beside the catalogue, with no data changed, leaves it alone (`174b24a`).
- When two branches took the same number, a later commit raised it again for the two together (`9346be9`).

So raise it by one when a change alters what the catalogue holds. Before it was written down, that was the usual practice rather than a rule that always held: pull requests #107 and #108 changed digging's numbers and removed a Design with no raise, and within a branch that has raised it, later commits sometimes haven't (`8c44cb2` added six Milestones).
